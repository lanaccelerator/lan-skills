# Revenue Bowtie: One Process From First Lead to Expansion

**When to use:** any skill that designs or audits how a B2B startup generates, closes, keeps and grows revenue: go-to-market plans, sales playbooks, retention and expansion, KPI systems. Referenced by go-to-market-strategist, sales-playbook-generator and customer-retention-expansion.

**Sources:**
- Winning by Design, the Bowtie model (Jacco van der Kooij): https://winningbydesign.com/bowtie/ . Seven stages: Awareness, Education, Selection, Commit, Onboard, Impact, Expansion, with volume, conversion and time measured at every stage.
- Winning by Design, SPICED qualification (Situation, Pain, Impact, Critical Event, Decision).
- Diego Morales, Rocket Revenue (rocketrevenuehq.com), "Revenue" masterclass for LAN Accelerator V5, Santiago, Nov 2026, and his public Revenue Essentials guides: the four GTM paths by ticket size, ABM and land and expand, outbound art and science, CS coverage model and CS manual. Founder of Rocket Revenue; former CRO of a contract-management SaaS and first marketing director of Start-Up Chile.

**License/Attribution:** synthesized from the sources above. Credit Winning by Design for the model and Diego Morales (Rocket Revenue) for the practice rules when you use them in output.

## 1. The funnel is a bowtie

The classic marketing funnel (TOFU/MOFU/BOFU) ends at the sale, and AARRR treats revenue as one more consumer step. In B2B that is wrong for two reasons: 2 to 7 people decide each purchase (Morales; Forrester counts about 13, see b2b-buying-2026.md), and recurring revenue is won or lost after the close. The bowtie lays the funnel on its side and adds the right half:

| Side | Stages | Owner | Job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Left | Awareness, Education, Selection | Marketing, SDR/BDR, sales | Generate qualified meetings and close |
| Knot | Commit | Sales hands to CS | Signed deal with the success criteria written down |
| Right | Onboard, Impact, Expansion | Customer success | Reach the habit moment, retain, upsell and cross-sell |

Rules:
- **Closing is the start, not the end.** The handoff from sales to CS is where most damage happens; write it down as a step with an owner.
- **Customer success is not support.** Support closes tickets. CS retains and expands. A CS team measured on tickets is a support team.
- **Upsell vs cross-sell:** more of the same product is upsell; a different product is cross-sell.
- **Marketing at early stage generates demand, not brand.** Its metrics are prospects, qualified leads and meetings. Positioning work that produces none of these waits.

## 2. Measure every stage: conversion and time

A process without a conversion rate and a cycle time per stage cannot be optimized: you do not know where to add or remove resources. For each stage record:

| Stage | Volume in | Conversion to next | Days in stage | Owner |
|---|---|---|---|---|

Example from the masterclass: 1,000 leads to 100 qualified (10%), 100 to 10 deals (10%). Find the stage with the worst conversion or the longest time and work there first. Weight the pipeline by amount, stage probability and expected cycle. Default stage probabilities (Rocket Revenue deck, replace with your own history): effective meeting 10%, demo 20%, quote 50%, negotiation 75%, contract sent 90%, won 100%.

The prospecting part of the funnel gets the same treatment: prospect, connected, meeting booked, meeting held and qualified won or qualified lost, each with its conversion and days. Lost-but-qualified leads go to a CRM recycle list with a follow-up date and the reason (Need or Timing), never to the bin.

**Typical sales cycles (Morales):** SMB 1-3 months, mid-market 3-5 months, enterprise 6-18 months. Use them to set expectations and the forecast, then replace them with the company's own numbers.

## 3. Qualification

- **MQL (marketing qualified lead):** qualified by behavior, not by a single action. Score combinations (registered and attended the webinar, downloaded and replied) rather than one download.
- **SQL:** sales spoke to the person and qualified the opportunity with a method. Use BANT (Budget, Authority, Need, Timing) for simple sales and SPICED for consultative ones.
- Discovery is listening, not disqualifying on the first call. Open with "¿qué te hizo tomar esta reunión?" and build the business case from the answer.
- People inside companies buy for three reasons: a better experience, convenience, or a solved problem (lower cost or more revenue). The business case names which one.
- LATAM note (raised by founders in the session): in family-owned companies (common in Paraguay) the general manager or a trusted assistant decides, so reaching them early shortens the cycle.

## 4. Motion by ticket size (ACV)

As the ticket grows there are fewer companies and each needs more specialization. Decide marketing (volume or named accounts), sales (one phase or two) and CS (pooled, assigned or by tier) together, never one at a time. Use this as a rule of thumb, then adjust for the market:

| Path | ACV per year | Marketing | Sales | Customer success |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Volume | Under USD 2K | Self-serve, product-led | Card on the website, little or no human | Product-led onboarding, tech touch |
| Velocity | USD 2-5K | Inbound: content, AEO, video | One phase: one person prospects and closes, by volume | Product-led onboarding; one person or a system serves every account |
| Efficiency | USD 5-15K | Inbound plus targeted outbound | Two phases: SDR books the qualified meeting, a closer closes | A named CSM per account |
| Effectiveness | USD 50K and above | Account-based marketing (ABM) | Face to face, relationships, several meetings | Segmented CS, weekly check-in for strategic accounts |

The masterclass gave no rule for USD 15-50K: treat it as a blend of the two rows around it and decide by the number of people in the buying group.

Keep a mix of account sizes in the pipeline: only small accounts give speed but no logos; only large ones give logos but a long, lumpy cycle.

## 5. Inbound: build content backwards from the purchase decision

Every buyer goes through problem, research, consideration (compare options and price) and decision. Build content in reverse order:
1. **Decision first:** landing page with a quote or contact action, case studies, pricing logic.
2. **Consideration:** an honest comparison against the alternatives and why you differ.
3. **Awareness last:** articles and posts about the problem.

Channels: **AEO (answer engine optimization)**, so every piece answers a question the buyer asks ChatGPT, Claude or Google's AI answers; short and long video (a phone, a USD 20 wireless mic and USD 15 lights are enough; an agency is not needed to start); founder personal brand on LinkedIn. Inbound is a flywheel (attract, engage, delight, promoters bring the next strangers): it works, but attribution is indirect, so measure what you can.

## 6. ABM and land and expand

Inbound is a trawl net: you catch everything and filter. ABM is a harpoon: you pick the fish first.
- **Pick 10 target accounts** you most want as customers and put the whole effort on closing them.
- Three scopes: **one-to-many** (a whole vertical, with content written for it), **one-to-few** (a sub-segment, for example only public schools), **one-to-one** (one account, everything tailored).
- **Land and expand:** close a small first ticket (USD 1K) inside an account whose potential is far larger (USD 20K), then expand by upsell, other teams or other countries. Write the expansion potential next to every landed account.

## 7. Outbound: the science and the art

**Science:** define the ICP and a **blacklist** of customers you will not take (distractions, pilots that go nowhere). Build lists of companies and decision makers by industry, country and role (LinkedIn Sales Navigator, about USD 80 a month per Morales; check current pricing). Load them into a sequencing tool and a CRM, get phone numbers, follow up every touch.

**Art:** the message. Sit down and think about each important prospect. A message that gets answered names the prospect's specific pain and peers you already work with: "Trabajo con compañías como la tuya. Entiendo que tienen un problema de [dolor]. Trabajamos con [pares conocidos]. ¿Te sirve una reunión para ver si te puedo ayudar?" Sharing a relevant news item about their competitor is a strong opener. If it does not work yet, change the message, not the channel.

Numbers to label as the presenter's, not as benchmarks: LinkedIn messages around 20% open vs about 3% for cold email today. **Roles once outbound scales:** a researcher (builds accounts and buying groups), an SDR (contacts, calls, books meetings) and a closer or consultant (runs the meeting and closes), each with fixed pay plus a variable tied to a different goal (accounts and contacts researched, meetings booked, customers closed). Agencies can take the high-volume, low-value part (list building, booking), only under close supervision. Drafts by default (b2b-buying-2026.md section 8); WhatsApp opt-in and LinkedIn automation limits apply.

## 8. Customer success coverage model

Segment accounts and attend each tier differently. The coverage model is what keeps revenue.

| Tier | Accounts per person | Touch | Cadence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Strategic | 1-5 | High touch: a named person, results review with valuable information | Weekly check-in |
| Mid | 20-30 | Low touch: assigned rep, WhatsApp open | Every 1-2 months |
| Long tail | Many | Tech touch: in-product messages, notifications, email journeys, alerts to a CSM when usage drops | Automated |

Run each account through Land, Onboard, Adopt, Renewal and Expand, with a goal, actions, one metric and the risk per stage: kick-off held and sponsor secured at Land; setup and training CSAT at Onboard, with any score below 4 out of 5 treated as a risk; first value as early as possible.

**Write a CS manual, literally:** what happens on day 1 for each tier, the contact frequency, the channels and the SLAs. A strategic account that gets weekly value is also the first cross-sell into its other markets.
