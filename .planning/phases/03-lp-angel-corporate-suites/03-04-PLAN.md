SKILL.md body — use corporate vocabulary throughout (NEVER VC/startup vocabulary):
- Step 1 Input Gathering: 7 questions in ONE message — company name + sector + size (revenue/employees), the business problem or strategic challenge being addressed, current innovation budget and governance model (central innovation team vs BU-led), list of innovation initiatives already running, openness to external startups vs internal R&D vs acquisition, geographic scope (LATAM-only vs global), and timeline for results (proof of concept in 6 months vs 3 years).
- Step 2 Apply Frameworks: Read references/digital-transformation-context.md for Open Innovation framework context. - Step 3 Workflow: gather inputs → map innovation gaps (what's missing vs the business problem) → classify each gap (build internally / buy startup / partner / license) → identify startup archetypes that fill each gap → score strategic alignment (1-5) and implementation complexity (1-5) → produce innovation opportunity map per TEMPLATES.md → flag innovation theater indicators → Próximos Pasos
- Step 4 Output: Innovation Opportunity Map table (gap | build/buy/partner/license | startup archetype | strategic alignment 1-5 | complexity 1-5 | priority), innovation theater indicators with [THEATER] callouts (e.g., "[THEATER] No internal champion named — pilots without champions fail 80% of the time per NESTA data"), LATAM context box, recommended next actions by priority tier.

- Tone: @shared/frameworks/editorial-voice.md — "This is innovation theater, not real corporate venturing. You have no internal champion, no defined problem statement, and a 90-day timeline that is insufficient for any meaningful pilot."
- Step 6 Próximos Pasos: 3-5 concrete corporate actions within 60 days. NOT "explore more startups." Concrete: "Appoint a named internal champion at VP level before contacting any startup."

Source: Digital Transformation 2025 (references/digital-transformation-2025.pdf) — Open Innovation chapter context.

Key data points for open innovation evaluation:
- 84% of Fortune 500 companies have a corporate innovation program; only 23% report measurable strategic impact (BCG 2024 Corporate Innovation Survey)
- Pilot-to-scale conversion: industry average 12% (BCG); top-quartile corporate innovators: 35%
- "Not invented here" syndrome accounts for 40% of pilot failures in manufacturing sector (McKinsey 2024)
- Time from pilot approval to first payment to startup: 4-6 months median in LATAM (vs 6-8 weeks in US) — procurement barrier is the primary startup partnership killer
- Chesbrough "Open Innovation" (2003) key model: Outside-In (absorbing external knowledge), Inside-Out (licensing internal IP), Coupled (partnerships and alliances). Source: https://hbr.org/2003/07/a-better-way-to-innovate

LATAM corporate venturing data:
- Corporates/CVCs represent ~25% of LATAM LP base (LAVCA 2025)
- Brazilian corporates lead LATAM CVC activity: Itaú, TOTVS, Embraer, Ambev each with active CVC arms
- LATAM pilot success rate: ~18% (vs 25% globally) — lower due to procurement friction and internal champion deficit
- Digital adoption by sector in LATAM: Fintech (85% digitized), Telco (72%), Retail (45%), Manufacturing (28%), Agro (22%) — source: IDB Digital Transformation Report 2024

**references/latam-context.md** (~50 lines):
LATAM corporate innovation operational benchmarks:

- Colombia banking sector: Bancolombia Digital Factory and Nequi unit as model corporate innovation structures
- Brazil: Cubo Itaú and Wayra Brazil as corporate innovation hub models — hub model outperforms internal labs in startup sourcing (2.5× more qualified startups)
