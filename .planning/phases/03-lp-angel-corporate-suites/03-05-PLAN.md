**references/digital-transformation-context.md** (~60 lines):
CVC readiness and governance subset — BCG + GCV Institute:
Source: Digital Transformation 2025 (references/digital-transformation-2025.pdf) — Corporate Venturing chapter.

BCG Corporate Venturing Framework (4 CVC models):
1. Captive CVC: Corporate owns 100% of the fund, manages it internally. Best for: companies with >$1B revenue and dedicated investment team. Risk: talent retention (market compensation vs corporate salary). Source: https://www.bcg.com/publications/2021/corporate-venture-capital
2. LP in External Fund: Corporate commits capital to independent VC fund as LP. Advantages: access to deal flow without management overhead, no talent competition. Disadvantages: limited strategic control. Source: BCG
3. Accelerator Partnership: Corporate sponsors accelerator cohorts (Wayra, Rockstart, 500 Global LATAM). Investment rights optional. Best for: smaller corporations (<$500M revenue) or early-stage CVC programs.
4. Balance Sheet Investment: Ad-hoc corporate development investment, no formal CVC structure. High coordination cost, not scalable.

GCV Institute benchmarks:
- Global CVC activity 2024: 1,800+ active CVC units globally, $80B deployed (GCV Institute)
- Median check size: $1-5M per deal, 10-20 companies per fund cycle
- Average CVC fund life: 7-10 years
- Top-quartile CVC financial return: 15-20% IRR (vs market index 7-8%) — but strategic value often outweighs financial return
- Source: https://www.globalcorporateventuring.com/

Intel Capital case study (LATAM context): Intel Capital's LATAM investments (2008-2022) demonstrated that technology CVCs with clear strategic thesis outperform generalist corporate funds. Key lesson: CVC investment thesis must derive from corporate core business, not adjacency exploration.

**references/latam-context.md** (~50 lines):
LATAM CVC activity and benchmarks:
- Corporates/CVCs = ~25% of LATAM LP base (LAVCA 2025) — growing from ~15% in 2020
- Active LATAM CVC units: Itaú Unibanco (Cubo investment arm), Embraer (EmbraerX), TOTVS (TOTVS Ventures), Ambev (Bees investments), Grupo Bancolombia (Innovation Fund), Telmex (Intel Capital parallel — telecom CVC)
- LATAM CVC average check size: $500k-$2M USD (vs global median $1-5M)
- LATAM CVC investment horizon challenge: Brazilian SELIC rate at 10.5% (2024) means patient capital opportunity cost is high — CVC boards face pressure for shorter returns than the 7-10 year horizon requires
- Speed mismatch: Corporate procurement/legal cycles = 4-9 months; startup fundraising cycles = 2-3 months. Corporate CVC teams that cannot match startup speed lose deals to traditional VCs.
- Most common failure: CVC investment committee requiring same legal review as a corporate M&A ($200k legal fees per investment). Successful CVC programs cap investment legal review at $15-25k.
