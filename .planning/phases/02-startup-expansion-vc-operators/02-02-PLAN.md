SKILL.md body (8 sections, under 500 lines):

**Step 1: Input Gathering** — Ask in ONE message:
1. Deck format: Has the user shared a PDF, link, or described slides verbally?
2. Stage and round: pre-seed / seed / Series A (benchmark thresholds differ by stage)
3. Target investor profile: sector-agnostic VC / sector-focused VC / angel / corporate VC
4. Previous investor feedback: Any patterns in feedback received so far?
5. Geography: LATAM-only, US-raising, or both?

If user pastes slide content or describes slides, proceed. If they only say "review my deck" without content, ask them to share slide content or describe each slide.

**Step 2: Apply Frameworks** — Read references/deck-evaluation-rubric.md BEFORE scoring. Apply:
- DocSend Pitch Deck Analysis (200K+ decks): Highest-attention slides = Purpose, Team, Why Now, Financials. Average investor: 2m 41s total — every slide competes for attention.
- NFX Teardown Criteria: Concrete data > assertions; specificity > vague claims; team story > team bios
- Sequoia Deck Structure: Read @shared/frameworks/sequoia-deck.md for expected slide-by-slide content

Score each slide 1–5: 1 = fatal flaw, 3 = adequate, 5 = investor-grade. A single 1 is disqualifying.

**Step 3: Workflow** — Execute in order:
1. Gather inputs (one message)
2. Identify which slides are present and which are missing
3. Score each present slide (1-5) using rubric in references/deck-evaluation-rubric.md
4. Flag missing slides as [MISSING SLIDE] — their absence as a default score of 0
5. Identify the single weakest slide (lowest score) — this is the #1 fix priority
6. Apply LATAM context from references/latam-context.md
7. Produce per-slide scorecard using TEMPLATES.md structure
8. Provide deck-level verdict + Próximos Pasos

**Step 4: Output Format** — ALWAYS use the exact structure in TEMPLATES.md:
- Per-slide scorecard table: Slide | Score (1-5) | Key Issue | Recommendation
- Deck-level verdict: Pass / Conditional Pass / Reject (with conditions)
- Top 3 priority fixes (numbered, specific)
- LATAM Context box
- Próximos Pasos

**Step 5: LATAM Context** — Read references/latam-context.md BEFORE final output:
- LATAM founders routinely miss: comparable local raises (not just US comps), FX risk section for USD-billing decisions, regulatory environment slide if operating in regulated sector
- LATAM investor expectations differ: family office investors value team bios differently than institutional VCs — local operator credibility and network > brand names
- Deck language for LATAM raise: Spanish deck to LATAM investors; English deck to US investors; bilingual slides create confusion — pick one

**Tone & Editorial Voice** — See @shared/frameworks/editorial-voice.md — You are a seasoned investor looking at your 50th deck this week. You do not soften critique. A weak team slide gets: "This team slide does not tell me why this team specifically will win this market. Bios without context of why these roles + this market = unfair advantage."

**Step 6: Próximos Pasos** — 3-5 actions. First action always targets lowest-scoring slide. Be specific about WHAT to add/remove/rewrite, not just "improve it."

**Sources** — DocSend: https://www.docsend.com/index/pitch-decks/ | NFX: https://www.nfx.com/post/what-makes-a-great-pitch-deck | a16z: https://a16z.com/the-deck/ | @shared/frameworks/sequoia-deck.md

SKILL.md body (8 sections, under 500 lines):

**Step 1: Input Gathering** — Ask in ONE message:
1. Company name and one-sentence description
2. Target market (geography + customer segment)
3. Top 3-5 competitors the user already knows (name them — do not assume the user is unaware)
4. The two dimensions on which they believe they are most differentiated (these become the 2x2 axes)
5. Stage and funding status (helps calibrate defensibility timeline expectations)
6. What moat(s) do they believe they are building? (network effects / switching costs / data / brand / scale)

**Step 2: Apply Frameworks** — Read references/competitive-analysis-framework.md BEFORE mapping:
- Porter's Five Forces: Threat of new entrants, Supplier power, Buyer power, Threat of substitutes, Industry rivalry — apply to the user's industry, not just direct competitors
- a16z defensibility framework: https://a16z.com/defensibility/ — moat taxonomy
- CB Insights methodology: Market maps organized by category, not by technology

**Step 3: Workflow** — Execute in order:
1. Confirm the two 2x2 axes from user input (or propose axes if user is unsure, explain rationale)
2. Place user's startup + known competitors on 2x2 (describe position in text — markdown cannot render graphics)
3. Research any obvious competitors the user didn't mention based on their market description
4. Build competitor profile table (Name | Stage | Funding | Model | Strengths | Weaknesses | Direct/Indirect)
5. Build differentiation matrix (Feature/Capability × Competitor — checkbox + brief commentary)
6. Apply Porter's Five Forces to identify biggest structural threat (not just direct competitors)
7. Assess defensibility — what moats exist today vs what must be built
8. Apply LATAM context from references/latam-context.md
9. Output using TEMPLATES.md structure
10. Próximos Pasos targeting defensibility gaps

**Step 4: Output Format** — ALWAYS use TEMPLATES.md structure:
- 2x2 competitive matrix (text description of quadrant positions, user in upper-right by design choice)
- Competitor profile table (7 columns)
- Differentiation matrix (startup vs each competitor, 5-8 dimensions)
- Porter's Five Forces assessment (brief, risk-rated)
- Defensibility analysis (moats built vs moats required at next stage)
- LATAM Context box
- Próximos Pasos

**Step 5: LATAM Context** — Read references/latam-context.md:
- US incumbents rarely have LATAM-specific sales motion — this is the asymmetry window
- LATAM-first vs US-expanding competitive dynamics differ: local entrant has distribution advantage while US player has brand/capital advantage
- Regulatory moats are more achievable in LATAM — local licensing creates structural barriers (fintech, healthtech, logistics)
- Language and culture as an underrated moat: support in Spanish/Portuguese with LATAM-native experience vs English-only US competitor

**Tone & Editorial Voice** — See @shared/frameworks/editorial-voice.md. Do not validate a weak moat story. If the user claims "our moat is our technology" without specifying switching costs or network effects, flag it: "[WEAK MOAT] Technology is not a moat — it is a head start. What prevents a better-funded competitor from replicating your technology in 18 months?"

**Step 6: Próximos Pasos** — Focus on defensibility gaps. Identify which moat to prioritize building next quarter.

**Sources** — Porter's Five Forces (HBR 1979): https://www.hbs.edu/faculty/Pages/item.aspx?num=195 | a16z Defensibility: https://a16z.com/defensibility/ | CB Insights Market Maps: https://www.cbinsights.com/research/report/venture-capital-market-maps/ | references/competitive-analysis-framework.md

Title: "Competitive Analysis Framework — Porter's Five Forces + Defensibility Taxonomy"
Sources: Porter 1979 HBR: https://www.hbs.edu/faculty/Pages/item.aspx?num=195 | a16z: https://a16z.com/defensibility/

Sections:
1. Porter's Five Forces — definitions + what signals HIGH vs LOW threat per force
