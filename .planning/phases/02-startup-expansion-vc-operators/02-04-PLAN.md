**SKILL.md** — YAML frontmatter:
```yaml
---
name: rapid-mvp-builder
description: >
  For STARTUP FOUNDERS. MVP design and feature prioritization.
  ALWAYS invoke when a founder asks what to build first, how to
  design their MVP, how to prioritize features, or which tech stack
  to use for a startup product. NOT for growth strategy or channel
  selection (use go-to-market-strategist for that).
metadata:
  author: lan-accelerator
  version: "1.0"
  suite: startups
license: MIT
---
```

SKILL.md body (8 sections, under 500 lines):

**Step 1: Input Gathering** — Ask ALL in ONE message:
1. Business model: SaaS / marketplace / e-commerce / fintech / other
2. Target customer: Who is the first user? (not the TAM — the specific first 100 users)
3. The one core problem: What is the single biggest pain the MVP must solve?
4. What do you want to validate? (hypothesis the MVP will prove or disprove)
5. Founding team tech capacity: Solo non-technical / non-technical co-founder with CTO / technical founder
6. Geography: Brazil, Mexico, Colombia, pan-LATAM, US/global?
7. Timeline: How many weeks to MVP? (determines scope constraints)

**Step 2: Apply Frameworks** — Read references/mvp-framework.md BEFORE planning:
- Eric Ries "The Lean Startup" — Build-Measure-Learn loop, MVP = minimum required to test hypothesis (not minimum viable product in the feature sense)
  Source: http://theleanstartup.com/
- MoSCoW Prioritization: Must-have / Should-have / Could-have / Won't-have
  Source: https://www.productplan.com/glossary/moscow-prioritization/
- Pieter Levels constraint-based development: https://levels.io/bootstrapping/ — ship with nothing, iterate with signal
- YC "Launch Now": https://www.ycombinator.com/library/4D-yc-s-essential-guide-to-go-to-market — the cost of not shipping is higher than the cost of a rough launch

**Step 3: Workflow** — Execute in order:
1. Confirm the one hypothesis the MVP must validate — write it as a testable statement
2. List all features mentioned by the user (may be long)
3. Apply MoSCoW to each feature — challenge every "must-have" by asking "what breaks if this isn't in v1?"
4. Tech stack recommendation based on business model + team tech capacity (from references/mvp-framework.md decision tree)
5. Build 4-week build plan — Week 1: core data model + auth; Week 2: primary user flow; Week 3: payment + core integrations; Week 4: polish + launch prep
6. Identify LATAM-required integrations from references/latam-context.md based on geography input
7. Produce launch checklist (minimum viable launch criteria before calling it "live")
8. Output using TEMPLATES.md structure
9. Próximos Pasos

**Step 4: Output Format** — ALWAYS use TEMPLATES.md structure:
- Hypothesis statement (one sentence — what this MVP will prove or disprove)
- MoSCoW feature table: Feature | Category | Rationale | Dependency | Est. Build Time
- Tech stack recommendation table by layer (Frontend / Backend / Database / Payments / Auth)
- 4-week build plan table: Week | Sprint Goal | Features to Ship | Validation Method
- Launch checklist (minimum criteria before launch)
- LATAM Context box
- Próximos Pasos

**Step 5: LATAM Context** — Read references/latam-context.md:
- Payment integrations are NOT optional for LATAM consumer/fintech MVPs — they are must-haves:
  Brazil: PIX integration required for any consumer product. OXXO + SPEI for Mexican e-commerce.
  Without local payment, conversion rates are 60-80% lower than US equivalent product.
- Tech talent stack: LATAM developers skew toward Node.js, React, PostgreSQL (similar to global stack) — avoid niche frameworks that limit hiring
- Compliance at MVP (not optional): Brazil LGPD (GDPR equivalent) requires data collection notice even at MVP stage. Mexico SAT electronic invoicing (CFDI) required for B2B billing.
- Label LATAM context: "LATAM requirement" not "(LATAM, Cuantico 2026)" — these are regulatory/behavioral facts, not benchmarks

**Tone & Editorial Voice** — See @shared/frameworks/editorial-voice.md. Challenge scope creep aggressively. "You have listed 23 features for an MVP. That is not an MVP — that is a Series A product. The MVP must be ONE working user journey from login to value."

**Step 6: Próximos Pasos** — First action: validate the hypothesis. Not "build the product" — "talk to 5 target users this week before writing a single line of code."

**Sources** — Lean Startup: http://theleanstartup.com/ | MoSCoW: https://www.productplan.com/glossary/moscow-prioritization/ | Pieter Levels: https://levels.io/bootstrapping/ | YC Launch Now: https://www.ycombinator.com/library/4D-yc-s-essential-guide-to-go-to-market

**SKILL.md** — YAML frontmatter:

```yaml
---
name: pricing-strategy-designer
description: >
  For STARTUP FOUNDERS. Pricing strategy and packaging design.
  ALWAYS invoke when a founder asks how to price their product,

---
```

SKILL.md body (8 sections, under 500 lines):

**Step 1: Input Gathering** — Ask ALL in ONE message:
1. Business model and product description (what the product does and who uses it)
2. Current pricing if any: What do you charge today? If nothing, what have you been thinking?
3. Customer segments: Do you have different customer types with different budgets?
4. Value metric candidates: What do customers get more of when they use your product more? (usage, seats, records, revenue processed)
5. Competitor pricing: What do 2-3 closest competitors charge? (model + entry price if known)
6. Business model: SaaS / marketplace / transactional / API / fintech
7. Geography: LATAM-only, US, global? (major impact on price point)
8. Stage: Pre-revenue (designing from scratch) / Early revenue (optimizing existing) / Growth (restructuring)

**Step 2: Apply Frameworks** — Read references/pricing-framework.md BEFORE designing:
- Patrick Campbell / ProfitWell value metric framework: Value metric = what scales with customer value, not what scales with cost
  Source: https://www.profitwell.com/recur/all/saas-pricing
- OpenView Partners pricing survey (annual): https://openviewpartners.com/2023-saas-pricing-survey/
- Sequoia pricing guide: https://www.sequoiacap.com/article/pricing-your-product/
- "Monetizing Innovation" framework (Simon-Kucher): https://www.simon-kucher.com/en/book/monetizing-innovation

**Step 3: Workflow** — Execute in order:
1. Identify the value metric (what does the customer pay for that scales with the value they receive?)
2. Recommend pricing model: usage-based / subscription (seat or flat) / hybrid / freemium+paid / transaction fee
3. Design 3 pricing tiers (Starter / Growth / Enterprise) with feature sets and price anchors
4. Build competitive pricing table (user's competitors vs user's proposed price)
5. Price sensitivity analysis (rough willingness-to-pay estimate given segment and geography)
6. Flag if current or proposed pricing is chronically underpriced (common in LATAM — address directly)
7. Apply LATAM context annotations
8. Output using TEMPLATES.md structure
9. Próximos Pasos with A/B test recommendations

**Step 4: Output Format** — ALWAYS use TEMPLATES.md structure:
- Value metric identification (the one metric that scales with customer value)
- Pricing model recommendation (with rationale — why this model vs alternatives)
- Packaging tiers table: Tier | Price (monthly) | Price (annual) | Key Features | Limits | Target Segment
- Competitive pricing table: Competitor | Model | Entry Price | Mid-Tier | Enterprise
- Price sensitivity estimate
- LATAM Context box
- Próximos Pasos

**Step 5: LATAM Context** — Read references/latam-context.md:
- LATAM pricing discount: 40-60% below equivalent US product is common and usually justified by lower customer LTV and willingness to pay
- BUT: LATAM SaaS is chronically underpriced — if unit economics are strong, raise prices before raising capital
- Currency denomination decision: USD pricing signals international ambition and provides FX hedge; BRL/MXN pricing reduces friction but exposes to devaluation. Hybrid: USD price, local currency payment option.
- Annual vs monthly: LATAM B2B often requires 3+ months proof period before annual commitment. Offer monthly at 1.2-1.3× annual price as default, not annual-first.
- Freemium in LATAM: Freemium conversion rates 1-3% in LATAM vs 2-5% in US — shorter free tier with more friction to upgrade is more effective than open-ended free

**Tone & Editorial Voice** — See @shared/frameworks/editorial-voice.md. Challenge underpricing directly: "At $29/month you are leaving money on the table. Your 3 case studies show $1,400 average annual savings per customer. You are priced at 2% of your delivered value. Raise to $99/month and validate that churn does not increase."

**Step 6: Próximos Pasos** — Include a specific A/B pricing test recommendation. Example: "Run a shadow pricing test: send 50% of new trial signups to a $99 tier page vs $29. Measure signup rate difference. A <20% drop in signup rate means you should raise prices."

**Sources** — ProfitWell: https://www.profitwell.com/recur/all/saas-pricing | OpenView: https://openviewpartners.com/2023-saas-pricing-survey/ | Sequoia: https://www.sequoiacap.com/article/pricing-your-product/ | Monetizing Innovation: https://www.simon-kucher.com/en/book/monetizing-innovation
