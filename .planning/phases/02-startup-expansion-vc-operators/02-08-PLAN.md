SKILL.md body (8 sections, dual-mode: GP draft or associate/founder review):

**Step 1: Input Gathering** — Ask ALL in ONE message:
1. **Mode selection**: Are you (A) drafting a new term sheet as GP, or (B) reviewing/explaining an existing term sheet?
2. Deal stage: Pre-seed / Seed / Series A / Series B (market standards differ significantly)
3. Instrument type: Priced equity round / SAFE / KISS / Convertible note
4. If drafting (Mode A): Company name, valuation ask from founders, round size, lead investor status (are you leading?)
5. If reviewing (Mode B): Paste or describe the specific terms you want explained or flagged
6. Fund context: Fund I or Fund II+? Family office LPs? (affects governance complexity)
7. Geography: Mexico / Brazil / Colombia / pan-LATAM / US (LATAM legal structures differ significantly)

**Step 2: Apply Frameworks** — Read references/term-sheet-draft-guide.md BEFORE generating. Then:
- Read @shared/frameworks/brad-feld-terms.md for ALL term definitions and taxonomy — do NOT paraphrase or re-summarize; direct the executor to read this file
- Read @shared/frameworks/safe-note.md for SAFE and KISS mechanics if instrument is pre-priced
- NVCA Model Documents: https://nvca.org/resources/model-legal-documents/ (standard US priced round templates)
- Brad Feld "Venture Deals" 4th ed: https://www.amazon.com/Venture-Deals-Smarter-Lawyer-Capitalist/dp/1119594820

**Step 3: Workflow** — Branch by mode:

**Mode A (GP Drafting):**
1. Confirm deal stage → load market standards for that stage from references/term-sheet-draft-guide.md
2. Generate economic terms with market standard position and negotiating range per term
3. Generate control terms with market standard position
4. Generate administrative terms
5. Apply LATAM legal structure considerations from references/latam-fund-context.md
6. Output full term sheet draft with negotiation levers

**Mode B (Review/Explain):**
1. Parse each term the user presents
2. For each term: plain English explanation | why investors care | why founders care | red flag version
3. Compare each term against market standard for this stage — flag aggressive terms
4. Summarize overall term sheet aggressiveness (founder-friendly / market standard / investor-aggressive)
5. Identify top 3 terms to negotiate

**Step 4: Output Format** — ALWAYS use TEMPLATES.md structure. For Mode A: full term sheet sections with negotiation levers. For Mode B: term-by-term explanation table with market standard comparison and red flag flags.

**Step 5: LATAM Fund Context** — Read references/latam-fund-context.md:
- Brazil: FIDC (Fundo de Investimento em Direitos Creditórios) structure for credit-focused deals; standard equity via Sociedade Anônima or FIP (Fundo de Investimento em Participações)
- Mexico: CKD (Certificados de Capital de Desarrollo) and CERPI for institutional investors; first-time GPs typically use simpler SAPI (Sociedad Anónima Promotora de Inversión) structure
- Family office LP governance: Simpler board structure acceptable (observer rights vs. full board seat) when LP base is family offices unfamiliar with institutional governance
- Currency: USD-denominated terms are standard even for LATAM deals. Local currency consideration only for deals where the fund itself is denominated in local currency (unusual at Fund I)
- Anti-dilution: Weighted-average (broad-based) is market standard everywhere. Full ratchet is red flag in any geography.

**Tone & Editorial Voice** — See @shared/frameworks/editorial-voice.md. GP voice in Mode A (decisive, knows market standards, recommends specific positions). Associate voice in Mode B (precise, educational — explains both sides, flags issues without editorializing on the investor's behalf).

**Step 6: Recommendation** — Mode A: Final term sheet ready to send. Mode B: Top 3 negotiating points ranked by impact for the reviewer's perspective.

**FILE 3: references/latam-fund-context.md**

Title: "LATAM Legal Structures and Fund Governance Context"

Sections:
1. Brazilian fund structures:
   - FIP (Fundo de Investimento em Participações): Primary vehicle for PE/VC investments in Brazil. Regulated by CVM (Brazilian SEC). Requires Brazilian legal counsel.
   - FIDC (Fundo de Investimento em Direitos Creditórios): For credit/receivables-based investments. Used for fintech credit funds.
   - Equity via SA (Sociedade Anônima): Standard equity structure for portfolio companies receiving VC investment.

2. Mexican fund structures:
   - SAPI (Sociedad Anónima Promotora de Inversión): Flexible equity structure for startups — allows SAFE-equivalent instruments.
   - CKD (Certificados de Capital de Desarrollo): Listed on BMV (Mexican Stock Exchange). Used for institutional LP capital (pension funds AFORE).
   - CERPI (Certificados de Proyectos de Inversión): Similar to CKD but more flexible. Both require CNBV registration.
   - Fund I GPs typically use offshore LP structure (Cayman Islands) rather than CKD/CERPI due to complexity.

3. Family office LP governance considerations:
   - Family office LPs (dominant in LATAM) are less familiar with ILPA-standard institutional governance
   - 3-person board (2 founders + 1 lead investor) is typical for LATAM Seed — avoid over-engineering governance at Fund I
   - Observer rights acceptable as alternative to full board seat when fund is not leading
   - ILPA reporting templates can be simplified for family office LPs who prefer quarterly calls

4. Currency denomination:
   - USD terms standard even for LATAM-focused funds — provides stability and avoids FX-linked disputes
   - Exception: Brazilian funds structured as FIP/CKD may have BRL denomination requirements for domestic institutional LPs

Sources: CVM (Brazil): https://www.gov.br/cvm | CNBV (Mexico): https://www.cnbv.gob.mx | NVCA model docs: https://nvca.org/resources/model-legal-documents/
