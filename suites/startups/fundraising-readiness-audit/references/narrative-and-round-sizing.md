# Narrative, Validators and Round Sizing (LAN masterclass, October 2026)

**Source:** LAN Accelerator fundraising masterclass for the LAN V5 cohort (October 2026). Benchmarks
cited inline. Label US data `(US-sourced benchmark)`.
**When to use:** dimension c (narrative), the validators check, and the "how much / at what
valuation" block of the scorecard.

---

## 1. The narrative test

The strongest fundraising story fits in one line: "We are growing X% per month and want to take the
market." If the founder cannot say it with a real X, the narrative is NOT READY, however good the
deck looks. Everything else in the deck must support that line.

- The earlier the stage, the more investors buy the future: team, vision, speed.
- The later the stage, the more they buy the track record: numbers, retention, margins.
- LATAM has low liquidity (USD 4.1B of VC in 2025, Crunchbase; USD 4.126B in 681 rounds, Cuantico
  2026), so team and growth weigh most when a LATAM fund decides.

## 2. Validators that weigh (the "logos")

Score each as present / absent. Cheap signals (letters of intent, free pilots, "we are talking to")
do not count.

| Validator | Counts when |
|---|---|
| Investors already in | A known fund or named angels have put money in |
| Advisors | People with weight have put their name next to the founders' |
| Closed customers | Signed contracts, paying. Not pilots |
| Clear traction | MRR, MoM growth and retention that survive diligence |
| Founder CV | Ivy League, a top company, a prior exit, or something extraordinary |

## 3. The growth bar

LAN's read: the bar moved. About 10% per month used to be the US standard for a good startup; the
top funds now look for 10% per week. Y Combinator already called 5-7% per week "good" and 10% per
week "exceptional" (Paul Graham, "Startup = Growth", 2012).

Compounding over one year: 1.10^12 = 3.1x (10% monthly) vs 1.10^52 = 142x (10% weekly).
PG's table: 1%/week = 1.7x/year; 2% = 2.8x; 5% = 12.6x; 7% = 33.7x; 10% = 142x.

## 4. How much to raise

Work backwards from the next round:

```
Round = monthly burn of the plan x (months to the milestones the next round requires + 6 months buffer)
```

Illustrative: USD 50K x (18 + 6) = USD 1.2M.

- Each round should buy 18-24 months of runway. Less, and the founder is raising again within a year.
- Milestones = what the next investor will ask for (MRR, growth, customers).
- The deck states where each dollar goes and which milestone it buys.

## 5. At what valuation

Start from dilution, not from the valuation:

```
Post-money = round / target dilution (15-25% per round)
Pre-money  = post-money - round
```

Illustrative: USD 1.2M / 20% = USD 6M post-money, USD 4.8M pre-money.

Median dilution per priced round, US: seed about 19-20%, Series A about 18% (Carta, "Startup
dilution per round is not rising", 2025, 12,435 priced US software rounds since Jan 2021). Across
seed to Series C it fell from about 18% to 16% during 2025 (Carta, State of Private Markets: 2025
in review, Feb 2026). All US-sourced benchmarks.

Sanity checks before the founder states a number:
1. **Comparables:** verified rounds at the same stage and market (see latam-comparable-raises.md
   section 3). LATAM valuations usually sit below US ones; no public dataset quantifies the gap, so
   do not quote a percentage discount.
2. **Next round:** the next investor must be able to pay 2-3x this price. An inflated valuation
   today is a down round tomorrow.
3. **The market decides:** the lead sets the price; competition between funds is the only thing
   that improves it.
