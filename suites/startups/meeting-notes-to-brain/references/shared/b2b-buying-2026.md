## 1. The core fact: nobody buys alone

About 13 people weigh in on the average B2B purchase, and 86% of purchases stall (Forrester). A deal with one contact is a deal with one point of failure. Every account plan names at least these roles, each with a name or `[PENDIENTE]`:

| Role | What they care about | What they need from you |
|---|---|---|
| Decider (economic buyer) | Budget, risk, strategic fit | One-page business case with their metric |
| Champion | Looking good, solving their team's pain | Material they can forward without editing |
| User | Will this make my day easier or harder | Demo on their own workflow |
| Finance / procurement | Price, terms, vendor risk | Clear billing unit, caps, vendor documents ready |
| IT / security / legal (when relevant) | Data, integration, contract | Security answers and contract draft early |

## 2. Multi-threading

Work several people inside the same account at the same time, coordinated, each with a message written for their role. Rules:

- Start with 3-5 people per target account (decider, champion, user, finance or procurement, plus IT when data is involved). Never one contact only.
- One account owner on your side coordinates the threads, so two messages never contradict each other or land on the same day.
- Personalize per role: the decider gets the outcome and the number, the user gets the workflow, finance gets the billing logic.
- Mention the others openly when it helps ("estoy hablando con [champion] sobre esto"), never as pressure.
- If the champion goes silent, the other threads keep the deal alive.

## 3. The talk-ratio rule: founder max 33%

In a sales meeting the founder talks at most 33% of the time. The client talks the rest. (Reference only: Gong Labs found top-converting calls around 43% seller talk. LAN's rule is stricter because founders over-pitch.)

Meeting structure:

1. **Short opening** (2-3 minutes): why we are here, what we want to learn, agenda agreed.
2. **Questions first**: the must-ask list below.
3. **Brief demo, only on their pain**: show only what solves what they just said. No feature tour.
4. **More questions**: reactions, gaps, the no-brainer feature.
5. **Close with a dated next step**: a meeting on the calendar, not "te escribo".

## 4. Must-ask list (every discovery call)

| # | Question | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | What tools do you use today for this? | Real competition and switching cost |
| 2 | Is there budget for this, and how much? | Qualification |
| 3 | Are you actively looking for this kind of solution? | Urgency |
| 4 | What other options have you considered (including doing nothing)? | Competition and status quo |
| 5 | What is your timeline? | Forecast |
| 6 | What requirements must it meet (technical, legal, security)? | Avoids late surprises |
| 7 | Which single feature would make this a no-brainer for you? | Scope of the pilot or Mínimo plan |
| 8 | Who decides and who approves? What is the process? | Decision map for multi-threading |

Coverage is scored after every call: questions asked / 8. Below 6 of 8 means a follow-up qualification call before any proposal.

## 5. Business case before the proposal

Write the business case in full sentences (not bullets) and send it BEFORE the decision meeting. Structure:

1. **Their goal and its owner**: the objective in their words and who owns it.
2. **The cost of today in their words**: what the current situation costs, quoted.
3. **Three options, including doing nothing**: status quo, an alternative, your recommendation.
4. **Their numbers, conservative**: use their figures; when you estimate, take the low end and say so.
5. **Risks up front**: what could go wrong and how it is mitigated.
6. **Recommendation and next step**.

## 6. The executive meeting

1. **Pre-wire with the champion**: they read the business case first and tell you the objections.
2. **Find who decides**: confirm the decider will be in the room.
3. **One page, 48 hours before**: send the one-page summary two days ahead.
4. **Open with the recommendation**: first minute, not slide 12.
5. **Maximum three reasons**, each tied to one of their metrics.
6. **Ask for the decision** explicitly.
7. **Leave with a dated next step**.

## 7. Full B2B process

Prospect → first touch → discovery → technical and integration meetings → proposal → legal and contract → vendor-portal onboarding → onboarding → invoicing and collections → retention → upsell → referrals.

**Vendor-portal readiness.** Large LATAM companies register suppliers in a vendor portal before the first invoice. Keep a folder ready from day one: tax ID (RUC, RUT, CUIT, RFC, CNPJ), certificate of good standing and tax compliance, insurance policies, bylaws, legal representative's ID and powers, bank account certificate, and the security documentation in use. A missing document can delay the first payment by weeks.

**Prospect through gremios.** Industry associations, chambers and clusters gather the ICP in one place. Examples, Chile: SOFOFA, CChC, SNA, ABIF, FinteChile, AACH, Clínicas de Chile, CCS, ASECH, CONUPIA. Paraguay: UIP, ARP, CAPECO, CAPACO, ASOBAN, Cámara Paraguaya de Fintech, CISOFT, AMCHAM.

## 8. Warm first, outbound that learns

- **Warm intros come first.** Find three "super whos": people with access to thousands of your ICP, across different industries or associations (Nájera). Script: "Hola [nombre], estoy ayudando a [cliente ideal] a [resolver X] con [solución]. ¿Conocés a alguien que se pueda beneficiar?" Nájera reports about 30% reply and 12% positive on his automated warm outreach.
- **Referrals several times a year**, asked at the "thank you" moment (after a win, a renewal, a compliment).
- **WhatsApp communities** where the ICP already gathers.
- **Cold email is a channel that learns**, not a last resort: drafts by default, the founder reviews the first 20, every reply is logged and classified, and what worked is saved as a learning in the company brain.
- **CRM the same day** for every touchpoint.
