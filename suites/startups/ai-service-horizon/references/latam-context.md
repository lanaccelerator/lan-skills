# LATAM Context: AI-Native Services in Latin America

**Sources:**
- LAN Accelerator operator read from LATAM founders (2023-2026), directional
- Brazil LGPD (Lei 13.709/2018): fines up to 2% of revenue in Brazil
- Paraguay Ley 7593 (general personal data protection), promulgated 27 Nov 2025 with a
  two-year grace period, enforceable around Nov 2027 (ferrere.com)
- For benchmark numbers, see shared/latam-benchmarks.md

**When to use:** ai-service-horizon Steps 4-9, when the startup sells in Latin America or
runs its oversight team from the region.

---

## 1. The service budget already exists

- **The outsourced professional is the norm for SMEs.** Most LATAM SMEs pay an external
  contador every month, many use an external lawyer or "gestor" for filings, and agencies run
  collections and recruiting. That monthly fee is the budget an AI-native service competes
  for, and it is usually larger than what the same SME pays for software (directional).
- **Software adoption is lower than in the US.** Many SMEs never bought the tool. Selling the
  finished work skips the "learn a new software" barrier: the customer keeps sending documents
  by WhatsApp or email, and gets the result.
- **WhatsApp is the interface.** Intake, reminders and delivery of results often happen over
  WhatsApp. Build the service around it (official WhatsApp Business API, with opt-in).

## 2. Licensed signatures and regulation

Several finished units require a licensed professional on record:

| Work | Typical requirement (verify per country) |
|---|---|
| Tax filings and financial statements | Registered accountant (contador público) signs or files |
| Legal documents, powers, corporate acts | Lawyer and, often, a notary (escribano) |
| Payroll and labor filings | Accountant or labor specialist; strict deadlines and fines |
| Health-related advice or reports | Licensed health professional |
| Credit decisions | Regulated entity, depending on the country |

Consequence: the HUMAN tasks in the work split include the signature, and the margin model
includes the licensed professional's time and liability. Partnering with licensed firms (they
sign, your agents prepare) is a common way to start.

## 3. Oversight talent and margins

- Loaded cost of skilled oversight staff (accountants, analysts, paralegals) is lower in most
  LATAM markets than in the US (directional). That helps service margins, but local prices are
  also lower, so the margin gain is real mainly when selling to larger companies or abroad.
- **Nearshore AI-native services:** a LATAM team overseeing agents can sell the finished work
  to US buyers in their time zone, priced against US labor. This is one of the strongest
  service-horizon plays available to LATAM founders; contracts, invoicing and data transfer
  terms must be ready for US procurement.

## 4. Data protection

- Customer data now flows through your agents and your people: least privilege per agent and
  tool, explicit consent, a processing agreement with each customer, and a record of where
  data is stored.
- Brazil (LGPD): fines up to 2% of revenue in Brazil.
- Paraguay (Ley 7593): promulgated 27 Nov 2025, two-year grace period, enforceable around Nov
  2027. Build the controls now; customers will ask in procurement before the deadline.
- Other countries (Mexico, Colombia, Chile, Argentina) have their own laws; check the current
  text with local counsel before signing data-heavy service contracts.

## 5. Billing and collections

- Outcome or %-of-savings billing needs a measurement both sides accept and a collection
  process: agree the metric, the data source and the monthly statement in the contract.
- Corporates pay at 30-60 days; a service that pays its oversight team monthly needs the
  setup fee and a fixed component to cover working capital.
- State currency and taxes (IVA) explicitly; pricing in US dollars to local SMEs adds FX risk
  for the buyer.
