# Pitch Content Rules (what the deck has to say)

These rules come from LAN Accelerator's pitch rehearsals and deck rebuilds: the points
that came up for most startups in a cohort, and every correction the LAN partners made
on the rebuilt decks. A jury that sees a dozen pitches in a row remembers one or two
things; these rules make sure those things are the startup's.

Use them when grading a deck or a pitch rehearsal and when building or rebuilding one.
Design and the AI-slop list live in [deck-design-rules.md](deck-design-rules.md).

## 1. The opening says what they do

- First sentence: what the startup does and for whom.
- One hook: a hard number, a one-line story, or the team when it is the strongest asset.
- Nothing technical in the opening.

## 2. One star client threads the pitch

- Pick the best client: how they work today, what the problem costs them, what changed.
- What the client gains or saves against what it pays, and the payback time.
- The same case runs through problem, solution and traction.

## 3. How the startup makes money, said plainly

- The business model comes before traction.
- With a commission model, state the take rate against the competition's.
- Traction gets its own slide: revenue plus the two or three KPIs that matter.
- The stage in one sentence ("we started four months ago and got this").

## 4. Team: one achievement per founder

- Founders in focus; the rest of the team in one line.
- One concrete achievement per person, with logos of where they studied and worked.
- Programs and accelerators grouped; only credentials that weigh for this audience.
- Present founders by what they achieved: titles read differently in each country.

## 5. One ask, one line

- "We are raising X to reach Y, because Z", in large type, with a visual.
- The verb tense states the round's status, so investors do not misread it: an open
  round is "We are raising" ("Estamos levantando"); "We raised" ("Levantamos") is only
  for money already raised and closed.
- The amount and the milestone it buys, 12 to 18 months out, plus the instrument.
- No use-of-funds breakdown on stage: that is for the one-on-one meeting.
- A single request. If they want customers rather than money, name the exact customer.

## 6. Show, do not explain

- Product screenshots, and photos of how the work is done today (the spreadsheet, the
  chat thread).
- Geography on a map: clients, regulation, expansion.
- Growth on a chart with the milestone marked.

## 7. Logos for everything that is text today

- Clients, partners and the team's origins as logos.
- Investors already committed, with their logo: they validate the round.
- The strongest, best-known clients go in the first slides.

## 8. Speak to the room

- Open with clients and brands the local audience knows; the rest smaller.
- A client the room does not know gets one line of context: its size, what it represents.

## 9. What stays out of the main pitch

- Gross margin: to a prospective client it reads as overcharging; it is a diligence topic.
- The detailed go-to-market and supporting numbers: backup slides for Q&A.
- "We use AI" as a differentiator: everyone does. Say what they solve.

## 10. Delivery

- Vary speed, volume and tone; key sentences slower and word by word.
- Close each slide with a full stop and bridge to the next.
- Look at the audience and rehearse with a timer: the slot is a hard limit (often 4 minutes).
- Two versions when the deck will be sent: one to present, one that reads without the founder.

## 11. Grading flags

When grading, flag each miss as `[PITCH]` with the slide number and the fix. A deck that
misses 1, 2 or 5 fails the pitch regardless of design.
