# Deck Evaluation Rubric — Per-Slide Scoring Criteria

**When to use:** Read this file BEFORE scoring any slide in a deck review.
Apply the criteria below to assign a 1–5 score per slide. A score of 1 on any
single slide is disqualifying — flag it immediately as [FATAL].

**Sources:**
- DocSend Pitch Deck Analysis: https://www.docsend.com/index/pitch-decks/
- NFX Teardown Criteria: https://www.nfx.com/post/what-makes-a-great-pitch-deck
- Sequoia Framework: https://www.sequoiacap.com/article/writing-a-business-plan/

---

## Scoring Scale

| Score | Label | Meaning |
|-------|-------|---------|
| 5 | Investor-grade | This slide earns the next meeting |
| 4 | Strong | Above bar, minor improvements possible |
| 3 | Adequate | Passes but does not differentiate |
| 2 | Weak | Technically present, fails diligence |
| 1 | Fatal flaw | This slide alone causes investors to pass |
| 0 | Missing | Slide is absent entirely — treated as fatal |

---

## Slide Rubrics

### Company Purpose
**DocSend note:** Highest-attention slide after Team — average 22 seconds on a
2m 41s total review. Investors read this first and decide whether to continue.

| Score | Criteria |
|-------|----------|
| 5 | One declarative sentence: Verb + what + for whom + outcome. No jargon. Immediately clear who the customer is and what they gain. Investor can repeat it verbatim after reading once. |
| 3 | Clear but wordy, or uses industry jargon that requires context, or buries the core value after 2–3 sentences of setup. |
| 1 | Mission statement language ("We empower X to Y through Z"), taglines that could apply to any company, or requires reading the rest of the deck to understand what the company does. |

---

### Problem
| Score | Criteria |
|-------|----------|
| 5 | Quantified pain (size + frequency + cost to customer). Clear articulation of who specifically has this problem. Explanation of why existing solutions fail. Includes a concrete story or customer quote that makes the pain visceral. |
| 3 | Problem described and quantified but lacks specificity about who has it or why current solutions fail. May be stated but not felt. |
| 1 | Generic problem statement ("companies waste time on X") with no data, no customer specificity, and no failure mode of existing solutions. Investor cannot assess market validity. |

---

### Solution
| Score | Criteria |
|-------|----------|
| 5 | Leads with the specific capability that ONLY this company has. Product shown (screenshot, demo, workflow) — not described. Clear 10x improvement over status quo. |
| 3 | Product described but differentiation not clear. Features listed but not why this approach wins. No visual evidence of the product. |
| 1 | Feature list with no differentiation. Product "to be built." Description so generic it could apply to 10 competitors. |

---

### Why Now
**DocSend note:** Third highest attention slide. Missing this slide is the most
common fatal error in LATAM decks — investors assume timing is arbitrary.

| Score | Criteria |
|-------|----------|
| 5 | Two or three specific enabling events with dates and data: technology shift (cost curve, capability inflection), regulatory change, behavioral inflection, infrastructure availability. Explains why this could not have been built 3 years ago and why waiting 3 more years cedes first-mover advantage. |
| 3 | Vague "market is ready now" or one enabling factor mentioned without evidence or specificity. Does not prove that timing is non-arbitrary. |
| 1 | [MISSING SLIDE] or "The market is growing" — not a timing thesis. Investors will pass without understanding why now is the moment. |

---

### Market Size
| Score | Criteria |
|-------|----------|
| 5 | Bottom-up calculation: ICP count × average contract value = SAM. SAM × realistic capture rate = SOM. Numbers defensible and consistent with the company's stated ICP. Year 1 SAM vs Year 3 SAM progression shown. |
| 3 | TAM stated credibly but SAM/SOM not calculated, or top-down methodology used without bottoms-up validation. |
| 1 | "Gartner says the market is $50B" with no SAM/SOM. Or market claim is the entire economy ("every SMB needs this"). No investor passes due diligence on a top-down TAM with no bottoms-up validation. |

---

### Competition
| Score | Criteria |
|-------|----------|
| 5 | Honest competitor placement — credible incumbents included, not ignored. 2×2 matrix axes reflect actual differentiation dimensions (not generic "price vs features"). Company position in upper-right is defensible, not manufactured. Direct AND indirect competitors addressed. |
| 3 | Competitors identified but axes are generic or manufactured. Company position is not compelling or is obviously self-serving. Missing obvious competitors that investors will name. |
| 1 | "No competitors" or a matrix where every competitor is in the bottom-left and the company is alone in upper-right with axes that were clearly chosen to manufacture that result. This is a credibility-destroying slide. |

---

### Product
| Score | Criteria |
|-------|----------|
| 5 | Screenshots or demo video showing the actual product. Architecture or technical differentiation explained. Roadmap with milestones that connect product development to business milestones. At least one customer outcome data point (retention, usage metric, outcome improvement). |
| 3 | Product described with some visuals, but outcome data absent. Roadmap is a timeline without milestones that reflect business progress. |
| 1 | Mockups that do not match a live product. Pure description without any visual. "We're building X" with no current product state shown. |

---

### Business Model
| Score | Criteria |
|-------|----------|
| 5 | Pricing model with rationale (why this model, not an alternative). Unit economics: CAC, LTV, gross margin, payback period. Revenue streams clearly differentiated. Why-this-model explained (contractual recurring > usage > transactional — show the investor you chose deliberately). |
| 3 | Pricing stated but unit economics absent. Model described without explaining why this pricing construct is optimal for the customer and business. |
| 1 | "We will charge a subscription fee" with no numbers. Or business model so complex that the slide requires a separate explanation. |

---

### Team
**DocSend note:** Highest-attention slide overall (25+ seconds avg). Investors
spend more time on Team than any other slide. This slide earns or kills
conviction.

| Score | Criteria |
|-------|----------|
| 5 | Each founder has ONE credential that proves founder-market fit for this specific company in this specific market. Story of why this team, not just what they have done. LATAM investors additionally need: local operator credibility, sector network, and evidence of distribution access. |
| 3 | Bios list experience chronologically but do not tell the "why us for this market" story. Credentials exist but do not connect to market. |
| 1 | Photos, titles, and alma maters. No evidence of domain expertise. No explanation of why this combination of people will win this market. "We're passionate about X" without operational proof. |

---

### Financials
**DocSend note:** Second highest attention slide (22 seconds avg). Most common
fatal flaw in reviewed decks: absent or incomplete financials signal the
founder has not modeled the business.

| Score | Criteria |
|-------|----------|
| 5 | 24-month P&L projection with three key assumptions visible (growth rate, CAC or acquisition metric, gross margin %). Current ARR/MRR stated. Path to profitability or next funding milestone shown. Burn rate and runway explicitly stated. |
| 3 | Projections shown but assumptions hidden. Hockey stick without inflection explanation. Missing current metrics (MRR/ARR). |
| 1 | [MISSING SLIDE] or 5-year projections with no monthly granularity and no assumptions visible. No current metrics. Investors cannot assess model validity. Missing financials in a Seed deck is a rejection signal. |
