# Customer Retention and Expansion

The post-sale engine for a B2B startup: what happens after the contract is signed, until
the customer renews, buys more and refers the next one. The sale is not the end of the
process; it is the start of the part that compounds. Keeping and growing the customers you
already have is cheaper than finding new ones, and a few points of retention move profit
far more than a few points of acquisition (Reichheld and Bain research, via Gallo, HBR 2014).

Built on the customer success discipline (Mehta, Steinman and Murphy, "Customer Success",
2016: the customer reaches their desired outcome, and you prove it), David Skok's negative
churn and NRR logic, Reichheld's referral research (HBR 2003), Andrés Nájera's referral
and growth-formula practice (LAN mentor masterclass, Nov 2026), and the B2B buying rules
in references/shared/b2b-buying-2026.md (many people weigh in, so retention is
multi-threaded too).

Nothing is sent. Every client message, renewal proposal, referral ask and collection
reminder is a DRAFT for the founder.

## Step 1: Input Gathering

Read the company brain first, following references/shared/company-brain-integration.md
sections 1-3: `list_skills` (the company's own customer review or renewal procedure wins),
then `search_brain` and `read_page` for each account page, contracts, success criteria
from the proposal, meeting notes, usage reports, invoices, and the price list with the
three plans. `walk_graph` from each account to its people, meetings and deals. Read the
CRM or the account sheet if connected.

Then ask ONLY for what is missing, all in ONE message. Defaults in brackets.

1. **Accounts:** list of paying customers and pilots, plan, MRR, start date, renewal date.
   [From the brain and CRM]
2. **Success criteria per account:** what they bought it to achieve, with baseline and
   target. [From the signed proposal; else `[PENDIENTE]`]
3. **Usage data:** active users, units processed, last activity, trend over 4 weeks.
   [From product analytics or the customer's own report]
4. **Measured results:** before and after numbers, with the source. [From meeting notes and
   reports; never estimated]
5. **People per account:** champion, decider, user, finance contact, and whether each is
   still there. [From the brain]
6. **Billing status:** open invoices, days overdue, vendor-portal or purchase-order issues.
   [From accounting or the sheet]
7. **Support:** open tickets and complaints in the last 30 days. [From the help desk or
   WhatsApp notes]
8. **Price list:** Mínimo, Estándar, Premium and billing components.
   [From `/pricing-strategy-designer` output in the brain]
9. **Scope of this run:** full portfolio review, one account's business review, renewals of
   the next 90 days, or a referral round. [Full portfolio review]

Mark anything missing as `[PENDIENTE]` and any estimate as `[ASSUMPTION: ...]`. Never
invent a result, a usage figure or a quote from a customer.

## Step 2: Onboarding to First Value

Read [references/retention-frameworks.md](references/retention-frameworks.md) section 1.

- Define **first value** per plan in the customer's terms: the first moment they see the
  result they bought (first report used in a decision, first recovered invoice, first
  suggestion accepted). Not "account created".
- Target **time to first value (TTFV)**: 14-21 days for a Mínimo pilot, 30 days for
  Estándar (directional). Track it per account.
- Onboarding checklist per account: kickoff with decider present, baseline signed,
  integration live, users trained, first value reached, first results check booked.
- An account past its TTFV target without first value goes straight to the risk list.

## Step 3: Health Score

Score every account 0-100 with five weighted dimensions (section 2 of the frameworks file):

| Dimension | Weight | What it measures |
|---|---|---|
| Uso | 30% | Actual use vs contracted scope, 4-week trend |
| Valor | 30% | Measured result vs the success criteria |
| Relación | 15% | Champion active, decider reached in the last 90 days, more than one thread |
| Soporte | 10% | Open issues, unresolved complaints |
| Pagos | 15% | Days overdue, invoicing problems |

Green 70 or more, yellow 50-69, red below 50. One dimension at 20 or less is a red flag
regardless of the total. Adjust weights only if the company saved its own in the brain.

## Step 4: Churn Signals and Save Plays

Check every account against the churn signal list (section 3): usage down 30%+ in 4
weeks, champion left or silent 21+ days, decider never met, results below criteria two
periods in a row, invoice 30+ days overdue, requests for data export or contract terms,
restructuring or budget-cut news, no-shows at reviews.

For each red or flagged account, write a save play: root cause hypothesis, meeting with
the decider within 7 days, a recovery plan with dates and a measurable target. Discounts
are not a save play by default: fix the value first. If the right answer is a smaller plan
(Premium to Estándar), say so; a downgraded customer is better than a lost one.

## Step 5: Renewal Calendar

Build the calendar for the next 180 days (section 4):

| Milestone | When | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| Internal review | T-120 days | Health, results, expansion option, risks |
| Business review | T-90 days | Measured results with the decider, renewal and upsell recommendation |
| Proposal | T-60 days | Renewal proposal draft, with the upsell option |
| Contract | T-30 days | Signature, vendor-portal update, purchase order |
| Renewal | T-0 | New term starts; invoice on day 1 |

Monthly contracts also get an anniversary review. Pilots get a conversion meeting 5 days
before the pilot ends. Flag every account already past a milestone.

## Step 6: Business Reviews with Measured Benefits

Prepare a one-page business review (QBR) per account due (section 5). Follow the executive
meeting rules in references/shared/b2b-buying-2026.md:

1. Open with the recommendation (renew, expand, or fix).
2. Their goal and its owner, in their words.
3. Measured results: baseline, today, change, method and source. Only measured numbers;
   conservative when estimated, and labeled.
4. What they pay vs what they get, in their numbers.
5. Maximum three reasons for the recommendation, each tied to one of their metrics.
6. Risks and what you will do about them.
7. The decision you ask for and a dated next step.

Pre-wire with the champion, confirm the decider attends, send the page 48 hours before.
The founder talks at most 33% of the meeting.

## Step 7: Expansion Plan

Use the three plans from `/pricing-strategy-designer`:

- **Mínimo to Estándar:** at the pilot's conversion meeting, against the success criteria
  agreed at signature, with the pre-agreed price and the setup credit.
- **Estándar to Premium:** triggered by measurable needs: volume above the included
  capacity, a new entity or region, a request for SLA, dedicated support or weekly reviews.
- **More of the same plan:** more sites, units or users as the customer grows.

Expansion math (section 6): revenue = customers x average ticket x purchase frequency. Three
improvements of 20% compound to 1.2 x 1.2 x 1.2 = 1.728, that is **+73%** (Nájera states
it as +66%; the compounded figure is +73%). Show the portfolio today and with each lever,
and which accounts move each lever. Hand off to `/unit-economics-calculator` for NRR and
LTV with expansion.

## Step 8: Referral Asks

Ask for referrals several times a year, at the thank-you moment (Nájera): right after a
business review with good numbers, a renewal, a compliment, an approved case study. At
least three asks per healthy account per year. Never ask a yellow or red account.

Script (adapted from Nájera): "Gracias, [nombre]. Con [resultado medido] que vimos hoy,
¿conocés a alguien en [otra empresa / su gremio] que se pueda beneficiar? Si te sirve, te
paso un texto corto para que lo reenvíes."

Make it easy: offer a forwardable two-line text, name the gremio or association where their
peers are, and log every ask in the CRM with its outcome. Referred leads go to
`/lead-to-proposal` or `/sales-playbook-generator`.

## Step 9: Case Studies and Measured Benefits to the Brain

After every business review with measured results, write back to the company brain
(section 7):

- `customers/<account>/resultados-<YYYY-QN>`: baseline, result, period, method, source,
  quote, and the permission level (public, anonymized, internal only).
- Append one row to `sales/beneficios-medidos` (industry, size, metric, before, after,
  period, permission). This is the page `/lead-to-proposal` reads to put refined, measured
  benefits from similar clients into new proposals.
- Never publish a customer name, logo or number without written permission. Anonymize by
  industry and size when permission is partial.

Keeping these pages current matters: in LAN Brain's workflow-bench (Aug 2026), fixing three
stale pricing pages raised scores 56 points across the four models tested. Stale benefit
pages produce stale proposals.

## Step 10: Invoicing and Collections Hygiene

(Section 9.) Invoice on the agreed day, with the purchase order number and in the vendor
portal when the customer uses one. Outcome fees are invoiced after the customer approves
the measurement report. Reminder sequence for overdue invoices, all drafts: day +1 friendly
note to accounts payable, day +7 with the invoice attached, day +15 copy the champion, day
+30 call and a pause rule agreed in the contract. Collections status feeds the Pagos
dimension. Never send a renewal or upsell proposal to an account with a disputed invoice
until the dispute is resolved.

## Step 11: CRM and Brain, Same Day

Follow references/shared/company-brain-integration.md sections 4-5. Every touchpoint is
recorded the same day: business review held, health change, renewal stage, upsell proposed,
referral asked, invoice overdue, churn signal. With a CRM connector: update stage, next step,
next-step date and an activity note, only for the accounts in scope. Without a CRM: update
the account sheet (columns in section 10 of the frameworks file) and list the changes.
Learnings about what worked (a save play, a referral line) go to the company brain, not into
this skill.

This skill can run weekly unattended to refresh health scores and the renewal calendar
(company-brain-integration section 6). The run writes a summary page and drafts only.

## Step 12: Output Format

ALWAYS use this exact template structure. Do not change section headings, table columns,
or field order. Fill every [bracketed placeholder] with real analysis from the user's
startup.

See [TEMPLATES.md](TEMPLATES.md) for the filled example. Sections, in order:

1. Resumen: verdict, MRR, accounts by color, the three actions of the week
2. Cartera y salud: Cuenta | Plan | MRR | Uso | Valor | Relación | Soporte | Pagos | Score | Estado | Señal principal
3. Señales de churn y planes de rescate
4. Calendario de renovaciones: Cuenta | Plan | MRR | Renovación | Próximo hito | Fecha | Estado
5. Plan de expansión: Cuenta | Hoy | Movimiento | Disparador | Fecha | MRR nuevo | Cambio MRR
6. La cuenta compuesta (1,2 x 1,2 x 1,2)
7. Pedidos de referidos: Cuenta | Momento del gracias | Fecha | Quién pide | A quién | Estado
8. Revisión de resultados (one-pager) for the account due first
9. Beneficios medidos al brain
10. Facturación y cobranza
11. Actualizaciones de CRM (same day)
12. Próximos pasos
13. Fuentes

User-facing output is in Spanish unless the founder works in another language.

## LATAM Context

See [references/latam-context.md](references/latam-context.md): relationship-first
accounts, the owner or Gerente General as the real decider, WhatsApp as the daily channel,
vendor portals and 30-60 day payment terms, referrals through gremios and WhatsApp
communities, and the year-end and holiday calendar. For benchmarks, follow
references/shared/latam-benchmarks.md and label LATAM ranges `(LATAM, directional)`.

## Tone & Editorial Voice

See references/shared/editorial-voice.md: direct, not a cheerleader. A red account is red:
say it and name the signal. "La Cuenta E renueva en 26 días, el champion se fue en
octubre y el uso cayó 35%. No es un riesgo, es una baja probable si no hablás con el Gerente
General esta semana." Do not call an account healthy because it pays on time. Do not ask a
customer for a referral before you can show them a measured result.

## Próximos Pasos

End every output with 3-5 concrete actions, for example:
- "Llamá hoy al Gerente General de la Cuenta E y pedí 30 minutos antes del viernes: el
  champion se fue y la renovación es el 01/12."
- "Mandá el one-pager de la Cuenta A al champion hoy para revisarlo juntos; la revisión con
  el decisor es el 12/11 y tiene que llegar 48 horas antes."
- "No mandes la propuesta de renovación a la Cuenta C hasta resolver la factura de 38 días:
  primero la orden de compra vencida con compras."
- "Cargá en `sales/beneficios-medidos` el -22% de merma de la Cuenta A, anonimizado, para
  que `/lead-to-proposal` lo use en la propuesta del jueves."
