# Pilot-Led Growth: method reference

LAN Accelerator's synthesis of the "Pilot-Led Growth" workshop by Sebastián González G.,
Managing Director of CIVLAC (IDB Lab and Wayra Hispam), November 2026. The ideas are his;
the wording, structure and examples here are LAN's. Credit him and CIVLAC when quoting.

## 1. Three approaches, three protagonists

| Approach | Central question | Who benefits |
|---|---|---|
| Corporate venturing | How does the corporate innovate with startups? | The corporate |
| Venture client | How does the corporate test or buy startup solutions as a client? | The commercial relationship |
| Pilot-Led Growth | How does the startup grow through corporate pilots? | The startup: acquisition, validation, revenue, expansion |

## 2. Why corporates

A hard buyer (long cycles, many deciders, compliance, hard access to the owner of the
problem) and a platform (real users and operations, data, channels, several countries,
budget to pay for a pilot). In a corporate open innovation program the pilot comes from a
prioritized challenge, then scouting, selection, pilot design and execution. Ask the
corporate what it already tried for the problem and with what results: it is the best raw
material for the hypothesis.

## 3. The pilot design canvas

| Block | Content |
|---|---|
| Company / Startup / Challenge | Who, who, the problem in one sentence |
| Hypothesis | What we believe the pilot will show |
| Metrics | What we will measure (SMART: specific, measurable, achievable, relevant, time-bound) |
| Goals | The minimum value that validates the hypothesis, over the client's baseline |
| Design: what, how, with whom, how many, when | The tested feature, the steps, the people, the sample, duration and milestones |
| **¿Y si funciona?** | What happens if it works; who decides and holds the budget; contract shape; what scaling needs |

The fourth row is González's addition to the corporate canvas: the success criteria become
the go / no-go decision contract. Next to the canvas, write the 2-3 riskiest assumptions.

## 4. Making the pilot cheap to approve

Fewer hypotheses, one area or unit, a small but representative sample, only the duration
needed for useful data, continuous feedback instead of a final report, partners who bring
resources, virtual training, cheap digital monitoring. Start small, prove value, then scale.

## 5. Promise vs hypothesis (example)

A queue-management startup promised large drops in waiting-room congestion and higher
satisfaction. The pilot tested one thing: average waiting time in 2 branches serving about
500 clients a day, goal -30%. The promise sells the meeting; the hypothesis sells the pilot.

## 6. The decision tree with the missing branch

Pilot validated? If no: declare the failure, pivot and redesign. If yes: the classic tree goes
straight to "integrate". Pilot-Led Growth adds the purchase conversation in between: who
signs, what contract. Without it the pilot lands in pilot purgatory: validated, nobody signs.

## 7. Founder metrics

Pilot-to-contract rate; time from pilot start to signature; share of pilots with decider and
budget named before starting (cited in the workshop from Siota, Camacho and Šerić, 2026,
UNIDO / IESE). Market reference, never a price anchor: a venture client POC costs EUR
75,000-100,000 with adoption up to about 50% in favorable conditions (GlassDollar, 2023,
Europe-sourced, directional for LATAM).

## 8. Case sheet (one per pilot)

Challenge; startup; hypothesis; experiment; metric; success criterion; result and decision.

## 9. The mindset

1. Discover the problem before selling technology.
2. Design an experiment aimed at adoption, not a mini contract.
3. Define success before starting.
4. Plan from day one what happens if it works.
5. Think beyond the first client.

A corporate pilot is not the destination: it is the starting point of the growth strategy.
