# Pilot-Led Growth

Answers: "How do I use corporate pilots to win a large first client, validate my value
proposition and grow, without ending up in pilot purgatory?" Pilot-Led Growth means
designing and managing corporate pilots as a structured mechanism for customer acquisition,
value validation and commercial expansion.

The method is the "Pilot-Led Growth" workshop by **Sebastián González G., Managing Director
of CIVLAC** (Corporate Impact Venturing Latin America & The Caribbean, a program of IDB Lab
and Wayra Hispam, Telefónica's open innovation arm), taught to LAN Accelerator's fifth
cohort in Santiago in November 2026. The ideas are his; the wording and examples are LAN's. Credit him and CIVLAC when the output quotes the method.

Five rules frame every answer:

1. **A pilot tests fit, not technology.** It measures whether the solution works in that
   client's context, which makes it smaller and easier to approve.
2. **A pilot is not a mini contract.** "Instalemos la plataforma 3 meses y vemos qué pasa"
   has no hypothesis, no agreed metric and no decision. A contract starts and ends; a pilot
   must trigger a decision.
3. **The contract is negotiated before the pilot starts.** Once it runs, there is no room left.
4. **Whoever arrives with the pilot designed leads the negotiation.** Corporates need help
   designing pilots; founders already know how to design experiments.
5. **Never start a pilot without knowing what happens if it succeeds.**

## Step 1: Input Gathering (one message)

Ask all of these in ONE message. Missing answers become `[SUPUESTO: ...]`.

1. Startup, solution and current evidence (clients, prior pilots, results with numbers)
2. Target corporate: company, business unit, country, who you talked to and their role
3. The problem you believe they have, and how you know (their words if possible)
4. What they have already tried for this problem, and with what result
5. Who on your team would work on the pilot, and what it costs you per month
6. Any terms already proposed by the corporate (free pilot, duration, data, tender after)

**Hard stop:** without a named problem for this client, write it as a hypothesis and ask the
founder to confirm it before designing anything.

## Step 2: Qualify the Opportunity (before spending an hour)

Read [references/pilot-method.md](references/pilot-method.md). Output a traffic light
(GREEN / YELLOW / RED) with one line of reasoning per criterion:

- **Urgency:** do they need this solved today? Important and urgent problems bring budget.
  Without urgency: six months of meetings and eight of piloting.
- **Problem owner vs entry door:** innovation is a very good door (if absent, new business or
  technology), but the owner of the pain is usually another area (risk, operations, plant).
  Name both.
- **Decider and budget:** can you name who signs and which budget pays?
- **Is it your problem?** If they want you to solve a neighboring problem B, or to hand over
  the technology, they are using you as a software factory. Options: find another counterpart,
  decline, or accept as a conscious decision that brings cash and credibility without losing
  focus.
- **Open-innovation calls:** a decider who only appears at the end of the process is a red
  flag.
- **What the corporate committee scores:** fit with the challenge and its KPIs; technical and
  adoption risks and how to mitigate them; maturity (idea, prototype, validated); prior
  adoption evidence; a team willing to co-create and iterate.

Not every startup needs to grow this way: it fits when there is a relevant corporate problem
and the solution can be tested in that environment.

## Step 3: Map Who Decides

Follow references/shared/b2b-buying-2026.md (buying group, multi-threading). The method's
four roles: who decides, who influences, who pays, who implements. Add the user, legal and
compliance, IT and security, and procurement when relevant. For each: their mini-problem and
what they need to see. Late arrivals (legal, the operator in the field) stall pilots.

## Step 4: Hypothesis

- Formula: **"Creemos que [solución] permitirá [reducir / aumentar / eliminar] [dolor o
  ganancia del cliente]"**, with how much, where and by when.
- Testable, precise (what, who, when) and discrete (one thing). Break it into 3-5 specific
  hypotheses and prioritize the ones that drive the decision.
- Pick the uncertainty the buyer worries about most and test it first: desirability (will
  they use it?), feasibility (does it work here?), viability (is the benefit worth the cost?).
- The promise sells the meeting; the hypothesis sells the pilot. From the promise, choose the
  **critical indicator** that triggers the decision. Measure many things, decide on one or two.

## Step 5: Design the Pilot (the canvas)

Fill the canvas in [references/pilot-method.md](references/pilot-method.md): company,
startup, challenge; hypothesis, metrics (SMART), goals over the client's baseline; design
(what, how, with whom, how many, when); and the row the corporate canvas lacks, **"¿Y si
funciona?"**: what happens if it works, who decides and holds the budget, what the contract
looks like and costs, what scaling needs (integrations, IT approval).

Design rules:
- **Small to approve:** few hypotheses, one area and a representative sample, only the
  duration needed for useful data, continuous feedback, partners who bring resources.
- **Duration:** 4-6 weeks is the workshop deck's reference; in the talk González called three
  months ideal ("en tres meses ya sabés si sí o si no"). A year is purgatory risk.
- **Success in ranges, not one number.** González's example, off the cuff: 15-25% improvement,
  test again; 25-35%, extend to another site; above 35%, buy. Set the real bands from the
  client's baseline; anything under the lowest band is a no.
- **Never shrink your standard contract to 3 months** and call it a pilot.
- Design it so even a "no" leaves evidence, fast and cheap. If it fails: declare the failure,
  pivot and redesign; do not stretch the pilot.
- The pilot is negotiated, not imposed: arrive with the draft.

## Step 6: Pilot Economics

- **Default: paid if possible.** Charge the incremental cost of serving this client during
  the pilot: dedicated team not otherwise covered, travel, hardware or assets. Not the
  founder's salary (González's personal view). A free pilot is a conscious investment with its cost calculated.
- **Free vs paid is not the critical point;** what gets measured and when they buy is. A
  pattern that works: free pilot with a pricier scale-up, or paid pilot with a cheaper
  scale-up; the corporate chooses (one option González has seen a founder offer). Where
  charging for the pilot blocks the sale (one workshop founder reported this in Argentina,
  a single data point), protect the post-pilot contract instead.
- **Break-even:** the result at which the client's savings equal the pilot's cost.
- **Payback:** months until the client recovers what it pays. Both need the client's baseline.
- Hand price levels to `/pricing-strategy-designer` and the unit math to
  `/unit-economics-calculator`.

## Step 7: Negotiation Checklist (closed before Day 1)

Mark each item agreed, pending or not applicable: what is tested, metric and goal ranges;
who decides at the end and on what date; contract shape, price, budget and budget cycle;
whether a tender follows (decide now if you will compete; your data from that same client is
the advantage); procurement knows about the pilot; data ownership, access, use, retention and
deletion (ask for data carefully: asking head-on closes doors); intellectual property of
anything built or adapted; confidentiality; use of logo, testimonial or case; and a technical
checklist (cloud, security, compliance, integrations) sent early for IT to answer.

## Step 8: The Pilot Slide

Corporate pitch competitions and demo days often pick the most charismatic founder, and then
nobody knows what problem they solve. Add one slide: "Así resolvería su problema y así lo
probaríamos con ustedes en [duración]": their problem, the hypothesis, the experiment
(where, scale, metric, goal) and what happens if it works. See
references/shared/pitch-content-rules.md.

## Step 9: After the Pilot

- Three possible results: conversion (they hire), expansion (other areas or operations),
  validation (evidence to sell to others, within agreed permissions).
- The ladder: A1 first pilot in one operation; A2 more features or areas (land and expand);
  B1 another client or country; the group network effect (subsidiaries, suppliers, partners).
- Success is not scalability: did you solve one company's problem or a market's?
- Document each pilot in a case sheet (challenge, hypothesis, experiment, metric, success
  criterion, result and decision). Track the founder metrics in Step 10 and hand renewals and
  expansion to `/customer-retention-expansion`.
- **"It went well and they do not buy":** review the "¿Y si funciona?" row, who signs,
  whether procurement knows and which budget cycle it falls in; then propose how to reopen
  the purchase conversation with the decider.

## Step 10: Output Format

ALWAYS use this exact template structure. Do not change section headings, table columns, or
field order. Fill every [bracketed placeholder] with real analysis from the user's startup.
See [TEMPLATES.md](TEMPLATES.md) for the filled example. Sections, in order, with these
headings: Calificación (one row per Step 2 criterion, plus the verdict), Mapa de quién
decide, Canvas del piloto (with the "¿Y si funciona?" row), Supuestos que pueden matar el
piloto (2-3), Economía del piloto, Checklist de negociación, Lámina de piloto, Métricas del
founder (pilot-to-contract rate, time to contract, pilots with decider and budget named on
Day 1), Próximos Pasos. Write the output in the founder's language and variant.

Other requests use the matching steps only: discovery prep (Steps 2-3 plus the questions in
references/shared/b2b-buying-2026.md), pricing a pilot (Step 6), "it went well and they do
not buy" (Step 9).

## Traps to Name

- Pilot purgatory: pilot after pilot ("ahora validemos esta otra cosa") for 6-12 months.
- The pilot that died of success: it worked, but the sponsor had no budget, procurement never
  heard of it and the next budget cycle was nine months away.
- Saying yes for the logo.
- Accepting a pilot you cannot later scale, produce or operate.
- Becoming the software factory for a problem that is not yours.
- The client keeps the learning: asks you to delete the data, then builds it in-house.
  Prevent it in the negotiation (data, IP, confidentiality), not at the end.

## LATAM Context

See [references/latam-context.md](references/latam-context.md). Regional holdings can carry a
pilot that worked in one country to the others; tenders and compliance rules shape what comes
after the pilot; vendor portals and local invoicing gate the first payment. For benchmarks,
follow references/shared/latam-benchmarks.md.

## Tone & Editorial Voice

See references/shared/editorial-voice.md. You are NOT a cheerleader. If the founder cannot
name who signs, say the pilot is not ready to start. If the success criterion is "they will
like it", say there is no pilot, only a free mini contract. If the corporate wants the
technology and not the problem solved, say it.

## Próximos Pasos

End every output with 3-5 concrete actions, for example:
- "Mandale hoy al gerente de operaciones el canvas con la fila '¿Y si funciona?' llena y
  pedile 30 minutos para acordar los rangos."
- "Antes de instalar nada, pedí la línea base de las últimas 8 semanas: sin eso no hay meta."
- "Mandá el checklist técnico a TI esta semana, no cuando el piloto ya esté corriendo."
- "Agregá la lámina de piloto al deck del reverse pitch del jueves."
