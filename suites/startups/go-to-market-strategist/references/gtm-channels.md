# Weinberg "Traction" 19 GTM Channels

**Source:**
- [Traction: How Any Startup Can Achieve Explosive Customer Growth](https://www.traction-book.com/)
  by Gabriel Weinberg & Justin Mares
- [Amazon (book)](https://www.amazon.com/Traction-Startup-Achieve-Explosive-Customer/dp/1591848369)
**When to use:** go-to-market-strategist reads this file when scoring channels for
a startup's GTM plan. All 19 channels are scored for each startup based on business
model, stage, and geography.

---

## How to Score Channels

For each startup, score each channel 1–3:
- **3** = High fit: channel matches business model, stage, geography, and budget
- **2** = Possible: worth a small experiment; not the primary bet
- **1** = Poor fit: wrong stage, wrong model, or LATAM channel performance too low

**Rule from Weinberg:** Every company finds 1–3 channels that work. The goal of
scoring is to identify which 3 to run experiments on first — not to find all channels.

## The 19 Channels

### 1. Viral / Word of Mouth

**Best for:** B2C products with natural social loop (messaging, photo sharing,
payments), B2B products with multi-seat use or public output (Slack, Figma, Notion)
**Stage suitability:** Pre-seed → Series A (but only for products with inherent
virality — not something you add to a product)
**Typical CAC range:** Near-zero (US benchmark); same in LATAM when working
**LATAM-specific notes:** WhatsApp group sharing is the primary viral loop in
LATAM B2C and SME B2B. A referral program that routes through WhatsApp (not
email) converts at 3–4× in LATAM vs email-based referrals. *(Operator observation)*
**Not for:** Products used privately (financial tools, HR software) where users
don't naturally share what they're using

---

### 2. PR / Media Coverage

**Best for:** Consumer products, B2B products with a strong founder narrative,
category creation stories
**Stage suitability:** Pre-seed (for credibility building), Seed (for customer
acquisition), less effective at Series A
**Typical CAC range:** Hard to measure; indirect; US benchmark: PR generates
0.5–2% conversion to trial for B2B SaaS *(US-sourced benchmark)*
**LATAM-specific notes:** LATAM tech media is smaller than US. TechCrunch Español,
Bloomberg Línea, Contxto, and Startups.mx have limited distribution vs US counterparts.
Local media (El Financiero, Expansion in Mexico; Valor in Brazil) reaches decision-makers
better than tech media for B2B products.
**Best use:** CEO profile stories in business press, not product press releases

---

### 3. Unconventional PR / Stunts

**Best for:** B2C consumer products with broad appeal, PR-able social missions
**Stage suitability:** Pre-seed to Seed
**Typical CAC range:** Near-zero if it works; unpredictable
**LATAM-specific notes:** Cultural stunts work in LATAM but require deep local
cultural knowledge — misaligned stunts backfire significantly. High risk for
international founders without LATAM cultural fluency.
**Not for:** B2B financial products, regulated industries

---

### 4. Search Engine Marketing (SEM / Google Ads)

**Best for:** Products solving a problem people search for. B2B SaaS, fintech,
e-commerce, healthcare
**Stage suitability:** Seed → Series A (requires budget; not ideal at pre-seed)
**Typical CAC range (US-sourced benchmark):** B2B SaaS: $150–$400/customer via Google Ads
**LATAM-specific notes:** CPC in LATAM is 40–70% below US for equivalent keywords.
"Crédito para empresas" CPCs in Mexico: $0.50–$2.00 vs $5–$15 for equivalent US
terms. Google is dominant in LATAM (90%+ search market share in MX/BR/CO). *(Operator
observation from LATAM SaaS campaigns)*
**Caution:** Requires conversion rate optimization. Budget without landing page
testing = waste.

---

### 5. Social & Display Ads

**Best for:** B2C consumer products (Facebook/Instagram), B2B executive targeting
(LinkedIn), remarketing for B2B
**Stage suitability:** Seed → Series A
**Typical CAC range (US-sourced benchmark):** LinkedIn B2B: $250–$800/lead; Facebook
B2C: $20–$100/customer
**LATAM-specific notes:**
- **LinkedIn:** CPM in LATAM is 30–40% below US equivalent. Effective for B2B SaaS
  targeting Finance Directors and CEOs in Brazil and Mexico. *(Operator observation)*
- **Facebook/Instagram:** High penetration in LATAM B2C. CPA 50–60% below US for
  same audience quality. *(Operator observation)*
- **WhatsApp Business ads:** Click-to-WhatsApp ads on Facebook are highly effective
  in LATAM for B2C and SME segments — allow users to message directly without email.

---

### 6. Offline Ads (TV, Radio, Print, Billboard)

**Best for:** Mass market consumer products with large budgets
**Stage suitability:** Series B+ only
**LATAM-specific notes:** Radio works in secondary cities in Mexico (radio
penetration is higher outside CDMX). Billboard effective in LATAM for consumer fintech
brand building. TV is expensive and prohibitive for early-stage.
**Not for:** Any B2B product, pre-Series A startups

---

### 7. SEO / Content Marketing

**Best for:** Products solving problems people search for; high-LTV customers willing
to research before buying
**Stage suitability:** Pre-seed to start building; Seed to double down; ROI peaks at
Series A
**Typical CAC range (US-sourced benchmark):** B2B SaaS organic: <$50/customer when
mature channel; 12–18 month ramp time
**LATAM-specific notes:**
- Spanish-language SaaS content is significantly underpopulated vs English — lower
  competition, faster ranking potential for LATAM-specific topics
- "Factura electrónica México", "crédito para pyme Colombia", "software contable
  Brasil" are high-intent keywords with low competition vs US equivalents
- Backlink building in LATAM is harder (fewer Spanish-language link exchanges and
  industry publications); focus on quality content over link building initially

---

### 8. Email Marketing

**Best for:** Products with nurture-heavy sales cycles, re-engagement of known leads
**Stage suitability:** Seed → Series A (for existing customer base)
**Typical CAC range (US-sourced benchmark):** Cold B2B email: $100–$300/customer
**LATAM-specific notes:**
- **Cold email deliverability is lower in LATAM:** .com.br and .mx domains have
  aggressive spam filters; cold emails to @empresa.com.mx often land in spam.
  Open rates for cold B2B email in Mexico: 8–12% vs 22% in US *(Operator observation)*
- **Warm email works:** Email to opt-in list, post-event follow-up, or inbound lead
  nurture performs normally
- **Recommendation:** Do not lead with cold email in LATAM B2B. Use WhatsApp for
  first contact; email for follow-up documentation and agreement sending.

---

### 9. Engineering as Marketing (Tools, Calculators, Embeds)

**Best for:** Developer tools, technical products, SaaS with quantifiable ROI
**Stage suitability:** Pre-seed (cheapest customer acquisition if it works) through
Series A
**LATAM-specific notes:** Works the same as US when the tool is genuinely useful.
Spanish-language calculators and free tools are rare — high SEO leverage potential.
Example: a free "¿Cuánto me cuesta no tener capital de trabajo?" calculator embeds in
accounting blog articles and drives inbound leads for a lending product.

---

### 10. Target Market Blogs / Niche Communities

**Best for:** Products with a defined target community (engineers, marketers, CFOs)
**Stage suitability:** Pre-seed → Seed
**LATAM-specific notes:**
- Industry association newsletters (CAINTRA, CMIC, COPARMEX in Mexico; FIESP, CNI
  in Brazil) have high open rates and trust with SME decision-makers
- WhatsApp grupos are the LATAM equivalent of Slack communities for SME professionals
- LinkedIn newsletters by industry leaders have growing followings in LATAM tech/finance

---

### 11. Business Development / Strategic Partnerships

**Best for:** B2B products that can distribute via an established platform or channel
partner
**Stage suitability:** Seed → Series A (requires legal contracts and integration work)
**Typical CAC range (US-sourced benchmark):** Near-zero direct CAC; high indirect cost
(partner management, integration)
**LATAM-specific notes:**
- Accounting software platforms (Alegra, Contpaqi, Siigo, Conta Azul) have
  100k–500k SME customers and are natural distribution partners for fintech, HR,
  or payroll products
- Corporate innovation programs (Wayra by Telefónica, Banorte Pitch, BBVA Open
  Innovation) provide enterprise distribution pilots
- Relationship-based BD deals take longer in LATAM — expect 3–6 months to first
  signed partner agreement vs 1–3 months in US *(Operator observation)*

---

### 12. Direct Sales (Outbound)

**Best for:** B2B products with $500+ deal values; enterprise SaaS; any product where
the buyer needs education before purchasing
**Stage suitability:** Pre-seed → Series A
**LATAM-specific notes:**
- WhatsApp is the primary outbound channel for SME segment in LATAM (see Channel 19)
- LinkedIn InMail works for enterprise segment (VP and C-suite) but has 2–3× longer
  response time than US
- Cold calls work in LATAM (less gatekeeping at SME level than in US enterprise);
  local phone number increases pick-up rate significantly
- **Warm intro is the master key:** In LATAM B2B, a mutual connection introduction
  converts at 5–10× cold outreach. Invest in building the network before scaling
  outbound. *(Operator observation; consistent with LAVCA 2025 founder surveys)*

---

### 13. Affiliate Programs

**Best for:** B2C e-commerce, financial products with easily-understood value
propositions, products with natural advocates (accountants, consultants)
**Stage suitability:** Seed → Series A
**LATAM-specific notes:**
- Accountant referral programs are highly effective for B2B fintech and accounting
  software in LATAM (accountants serve as trusted advisors to their SME clients)
- Affiliate payouts: in LATAM, percentage-of-transaction (1–3%) outperforms flat
  fee for financial products — affiliate is motivated by loan volume, not just signups
- Track via WhatsApp — affiliates in LATAM typically prefer WhatsApp coordination
  over email-based affiliate portals *(Operator observation)*

---

### 14. Existing Platforms (App Stores, Marketplaces)

**Best for:** Consumer apps, developer tools, e-commerce sellers
**Stage suitability:** Pre-seed → Series A
**LATAM-specific notes:**
- App Store / Google Play: Brazil has the 4th largest app economy globally; Mexico
  is top-10. Mobile-first products see significant organic discovery here.
- MercadoLibre ecosystem: for e-commerce and fintech, MercadoPago integration
  provides distribution to 40M+ active LATAM buyers — a platform-within-a-platform opportunity
- Accounting software marketplaces (Alegra App Store, Quickbooks Marketplace) are
  emerging as distribution channels for B2B SaaS add-ons in LATAM

---

### 15. Trade Shows / Industry Events

**Best for:** B2B products where face-to-face relationship initiation matters
**Stage suitability:** Seed → Series A
**LATAM-specific notes:**
- LATAM industry trade shows with SME decision-maker density: CAINTRA annual
  congress (Monterrey), Expo ANTAD (retail, Guadalajara), Expo Fintech (Mexico City),
  Febraban (Brazil banking sector)
- Decision-making happens in person in LATAM B2B at a higher rate than in US — trade
  show follow-up with WhatsApp (same day) converts 2–3× better than email follow-up
- Sponsoring smaller vertical association events ($500–$2,000) reaches highly
  qualified SME owners at a lower cost than large national expos

---

### 16. Offline Events / Workshops

**Best for:** Any B2B product targeting SME owners or professionals
**Stage suitability:** Pre-seed → Seed
**LATAM-specific notes:**
- Hosting a free workshop ("Cómo conseguir capital para PYMEs en 2026") through a
  CAINTRA or COPARMEX branch is a proven LATAM GTM tactic for B2B financial products
- Event marketing in LATAM builds trust faster than digital ads for regulated
  products (fintech, insurtech) where credibility is the primary objection
- Invite local accountants as speakers — they bring their SME clients to the event

---

### 17. Speaking Engagements

**Best for:** Founders building thought leadership; longer-term brand building
**Stage suitability:** Seed → Series A+ (too slow for pre-seed GTM)
**LATAM-specific notes:** LinkedIn is the primary speaking opportunity surface in
LATAM tech. Regional events (Talent Land, Campus Party, Startups MX) have founder
keynote slots. Generally a brand-building tool, not a primary acquisition channel.

---

### 18. Community Building

**Best for:** Products with strong network effects or community-driven growth
(developer tools, marketplaces, professional communities)
**Stage suitability:** Pre-seed → Series A
**LATAM-specific notes:**
- WhatsApp grupos and Telegram channels are the dominant community formats in LATAM
  (not Discord or Slack for non-technical audiences)
- Facebook Groups still have high engagement for SME professional communities in LATAM
  (vs US where Facebook Groups are declining)
- Building a WhatsApp community of 100 target customers is a legitimate pre-seed GTM
  strategy in LATAM — it's a closed-loop focus group and distribution channel combined

---

### 19. WhatsApp / Direct Messaging Outreach

**Best for:** B2B SME segment in LATAM; any product targeting mobile-first users
**Stage suitability:** Pre-seed → Seed (most effective; scales by volume)
**Typical CAC range (LATAM):** $20–$100 when effective (based on founder time cost
and conversion rates in LATAM B2B SME) *(Operator observation)*
**LATAM-specific notes:**
- **WhatsApp penetration:** 95%+ in Mexico, Brazil, Colombia *(GSMA 2024)*
- **Open rate:** 85%+ in B2B contexts vs 22% for cold email *(Operator observation;
  aligned with WhatsApp for Business benchmarks)*
- **Protocol:** Never send a link in the first message. First message = qualification
  question or shared context. Second message = soft pitch. Third message = call to action.
- **WhatsApp Business API:** For volumes >100 messages/day, use the official API to
  avoid account blocks. Providers: Twilio, Vonage, Meta Business Suite
- This channel has no US equivalent at this cost point — US founders underestimate
  how effective WhatsApp outreach is in LATAM B2B. It is Channel 12 (Direct Sales)
  delivered through the most-used communication platform in the region.

**Note on Weinberg taxonomy:** Weinberg's 2014 book predates WhatsApp for Business.
For LATAM, treat WhatsApp Outreach as a subset of Channel 12 (Direct Sales) with
LATAM-specific performance characteristics. This file tracks it separately given
its dominance in the LATAM context.

## Channel Selection Framework (Summary)

| Business Type | Stage | Top 3 Channels to Test First |
|--------------|-------|------------------------------|
| B2B SaaS | Pre-seed | WhatsApp outreach, Direct sales, Partnerships |
| B2B SaaS | Seed | Direct sales, Content/SEO, Partnerships |
| B2B SaaS | Series A | Direct sales, SEM, Partnership/platform |
| B2C / Consumer | Pre-seed | Viral/WOM, Social ads, Community |
| B2C / Consumer | Seed | Social ads, Content/SEO, Affiliate |
| B2B Fintech | Pre-seed | WhatsApp outreach, Accountant partners, Events |
| B2B Fintech | Seed | WhatsApp outreach, Accountant partners, SEM |
| Marketplace | Pre-seed | Direct sales (both sides), WOM, Offline events |
| E-commerce | Seed | Social ads, SEO/Content, Platform (MercadoLibre) |

Source: Adapted from Weinberg & Mares "Traction" (2014) with LATAM-specific
annotations *(operator observations from LATAM GTM practice, 2023–2026)*
