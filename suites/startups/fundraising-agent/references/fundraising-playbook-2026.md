# Fundraising Playbook (LAN masterclass, October 2026)

**Source:** LAN Accelerator fundraising masterclass for the LAN V5 cohort (October 2026), built on
the lore.vc 3-phase framework (CC BY-NC-SA 4.0) plus the benchmarks cited inline.
**When to use:** load in Step 1b (where to raise), Phase A (narrative, sizing, list of 30, scoring,
thesis and conflict check) and Phase B (lead investor). Every number keeps its source in the output.

---

## 0. Where to raise: LATAM or US

Same rules, different market. Decide before building the list.

| Question | Answer | Route |
|---|---|---|
| Is the product built for LATAM customers? | Yes | Raise in LATAM. This is most founders. Funds understand the market, the customers and the numbers. |
| Can you AND do you want to win the US market? | Yes, with a real plan to sell in the US | Go to the US: Delaware C-Corp, real presence, a network of backed founders who can intro you. |

Rule: raise where your market is. Going to the US without a plan to sell to the US is expensive tourism.

| | US | LATAM |
|---|---|---|
| Capital | Deep, thousands of funds | Low liquidity: USD 4.1B of VC in 2025 (Crunchbase); USD 4.126B in 681 rounds (Cuantico 2026) |
| What weighs most | Traction, market, track record | Team and growth, above everything |
| Tickets and valuations | Carta medians (see startup-valuation-benchmark) | Smaller, lower valuations |
| Exits | Many buyers | Few, almost always M&A |

## 1. A round has a beginning and an end

| Pattern | What happens | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Never raises | Never runs a process, waits for capital to show up | Most startups |
| Always raising | The round never closes; the market notices and the founder stops building | Avoid |
| Drip raises | Loose checks, valuation inches up round after round, messy cap table | Frowned upon by investors |
| A process | Open, create competition, close, go back to building | The healthy one |

## 2. The 9-month map (+3 months for LATAM)

| Phase | Months | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| I. Preparation | 1-3 | Narrative, how much and at what valuation, deck + data room + validators, list of 30 with thesis fit, warm intro map, gauge each fund's hunger |
| II. Fundraising | 4-6 | Convert the favorite into the lead, the switch (cohort in 1 week), parallel processes, follow-up D1/7/14/21 |
| III. Closing | 7-9 | Term sheet, due diligence (riskiest phase), legal, money in the bank |
| LATAM extra | 10-12 | Flip, wires, distance. Start with 15 months of runway, not 12 |

## 3. Phase I: preparation

**Narrative.** The best fundraising story is: "We are growing X% per month and want to take the
market." Everything else must support that line. The earlier the stage, the more investors buy the
future (team, vision, speed); the later, the more they buy the track record (numbers, retention,
margins).

**How much and at what valuation.** Run fundraising-readiness-audit
(references/narrative-and-round-sizing.md) for the formula: monthly burn x (months to next-round
milestones + 6 buffer), then post-money = round / target dilution of 15-25%.

**Validators (the "logos") that weigh:**
1. Investors already in (a known fund, named angels)
2. Advisors with weight who put their name next to yours
3. Closed, paying customers (signed contracts, not pilots or letters of intent)
4. Clear traction numbers (MRR, MoM growth, retention that survive diligence)
5. Founder CV (Ivy League, a top company, a prior exit, something extraordinary)

**Two tools, two jobs.**
- A CRM in Google Sheets holds the state: one row per fund (fund, tier, status, ticket, next step,
  date). The whole team sees it at a glance.
- LAN Brain holds everything in between: what each partner said (with the source), objections and
  how you answered, which material went to whom, each fund's thesis/portfolio/conflicts, and the
  round's decisions and why. The AI reads both, updates the CRM and preps each meeting.

**Where the list comes from.** Association directories are not the investor universe. LAN's
October 2026 survey counted about 760 declared members across 12 LATAM VC/PE associations (many are
PE, corporates, multilaterals or service providers), while a database of investors active in LATAM
shows more investors headquartered in North America than in South America (about 1.7 to 1). Build
the list from active investors, not only from the local association.

## 4. The list of 30 and the 5-variable score (100 points)

| Variable | Points | What it measures |
|---|---|---|
| Thesis fit | 30 | Do you fit its geography, stage, vertical and ticket? |
| Warmth | 25 | How warm is your access: intro from a founder in its portfolio > an acquaintance > cold |
| Stage and ticket | 20 | Does its typical check fit your round? |
| LATAM track | 15 | Has it invested in LATAM? Does it understand your market? |
| Access | 10 | Do you reach a partner, or only an analyst? |

Tiers: T1 80+, T2 60-79, T3 40-59, T4 under 40.

**Thesis: the filter before the filter.** Every fund promised its LPs where, when and in what it
invests (it is in the LPA). Check the four variables before the first email. Public example, LAN
Accelerator: geography LATAM; stage pre-seed and seed with traction; generalist, AI-native; tickets
of USD 25K to 50K.

**Portfolio conflict check (mandatory).** A fund almost never invests in two direct competitors; it
owes loyalty to the company it already backs, and your deck and metrics can end up in front of your
competitor. Mark "Do not apply" when the portfolio holds a direct competitor, even if the four thesis
variables fit.

**Conversion.** Warm intro converts roughly 25-40% to a meeting; cold email roughly 1-3% (LAN masterclass working benchmark, not a published dataset). From
LATAM, cold to a US fund converts close to zero.

## 5. Phase II: the lead investor

- Phase I is to gauge hunger: which funds want you, and which ones you want.
- Phase II is to convert your favorite into the lead. The lead usually takes about 50% of the
  round, comes in first and sets price and terms. Followers close once there is a lead. Finding the
  lead is the hardest and most crucial part of the round.
- The switch: contact all targets within 1 week; first meetings inside the same 2 weeks.
- Signing ASAP: a signed term sheet creates honest urgency. Script: "We have a term sheet, we have
  10 days, we would rather close with you. Can we speed up?"
- Classic trap: "I'm in once you have a lead." If everyone waits for the lead, there is no round.

## 6. Phase III: closing

Term sheet, due diligence, legal, money in the bank. It is not closed until the wire arrives.
Due diligence kills more rounds than outreach. A bad term sheet weighs 7-10 years. LATAM wires take
3-7 business days; put them in the timeline. The #1 DD failure in LATAM is an unsigned IP
assignment.

## 7. How investment committees decide (context for Phase II)

- About 100 deals considered per deal closed; 83 days on average to close; 118 hours of due
  diligence; team cited as the #1 factor by 47% of VCs; 30% of deals sourced from the partner's
  network vs 10% from founder inbound (Gompers, Gornall, Kaplan and Strebulaev, "How Do Venture
  Capitalists Make Decisions?", JFE 2020, survey of 885 VCs).
- The deal is voted in a room you are not in. Arm your champion partner with the arguments and the
  answers to objections before the IC.
