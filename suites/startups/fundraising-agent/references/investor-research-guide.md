# Investor Research Guide

**When to use:** fundraising-agent Phase B — read before preparing for any investor meeting.
Research every investor before the first meeting and before each follow-up.

---

## Why Investor Research Matters

Founders who arrive at investor meetings without having researched the investor's:
(1) recent portfolio, (2) stated thesis, and (3) known deal terms are signaling two things:
they cannot do basic due diligence, and they do not care enough to prepare. Investors
meet 200+ startups per year. The founders who stand out research the investor's portfolio,
identify a specific thesis fit angle, and arrive with a question that shows they've been
paying attention.

The goal is not to flatter the investor. The goal is to confirm this investor's thesis
genuinely fits your startup — and if it does, explain specifically why.

## Investor Research Toolkit

### NFX Signal

**URL:** https://signal.nfx.com
**Best for:** LATAM-relevant VC fund research — portfolio overlap checking, thesis mapping,
warm intro path identification

**How to use:**
1. Search investor name → see portfolio companies → check for direct conflicts
2. Use "Co-investors" tab to find funds that co-invest with target fund → expand your list
3. Use "Notable LPs" to find LP relationships for warm intro paths into funds
4. Signal Score shows investor activity level — prefer active investors (score >60)
   for this round; avoid inactive investors (score <20) who are harvest-mode

**Portfolio conflict check (critical step):**
Before approaching any investor, verify their portfolio does not include a direct
competitor. A fund with a competitor in portfolio will not invest in you (conflict of
interest) AND may share your information with the portfolio company. Use NFX Signal
portfolio tab as first step.

### Crunchbase

**URL:** https://www.crunchbase.com
**Best for:** Round history, check sizes, co-investors, and founding dates

**How to use:**
1. Search fund name → see all investments → filter by stage and year
2. Look at last 6 months of investments — is this fund actively deploying?
3. Check round sizes → infer check size range (lead check is typically 20–40% of round)
4. Look at co-investors → identify who they like to work with → expand your intro network

**Key question:** When was their last investment? If >12 months, the fund may be in
harvest mode or raising their next fund. Confirming fund status before outreach saves
wasted time.

### LinkedIn

**URL:** https://www.linkedin.com
**Best for:** Mutual connection mapping, GP background research, warm intro path identification

**How to use:**
1. Look up the specific GP/partner, not the fund — funds don't make decisions, people do
2. Check mutual connections for warm intro paths
3. Read their posts from the last 3 months — what sectors are they excited about?
4. Check where they worked before VC — industry background informs thesis bias

**GP background patterns:**
- Ex-founder → understands founder struggle, less process-heavy, moves faster
- Ex-banker/consultant → more analytical, process-heavy, needs clear metrics
- Ex-operator at big tech → thesis bias toward enterprise SaaS, technical depth
- Career VC → pattern-match-heavy; know the exact company archetype they invest in

### Latitud Scout

**URL:** https://scout.latitud.com (LATAM-specific)
**Best for:** LATAM VC fund profiles, LP relationships, fund size and stage data

**How to use:**
1. Search fund or investor name → LATAM-specific profile with fund details
2. LP relationships may show family office or institutional LP names → warm intro path
3. Stage focus is more accurate for LATAM funds than Crunchbase (which often has gaps)

### Fund Website and LP Letters

**Best for:** Stated thesis, most recent annual/quarterly letters (if public)

Many LATAM funds publish thesis essays. Read them before every meeting. Note specific
language from the thesis — reference it in your meeting preparation.

## Per-Investor Meeting Prep Brief

Produce this brief for every confirmed investor meeting. Deliver to founding team
48 hours before the meeting.

**Template:**

```
## Meeting Prep: [Investor Name], [Firm]
**Date:** [Meeting Date]
**Format:** [In-person / Video call]

### Their thesis (in their own words)
[Pull from fund website or recent public statement — not your interpretation]

### Recent investments (last 6 months)
- [Company 1] — [stage] — [why this fits their thesis]
- [Company 2] — [stage] — [why this fits their thesis]

### Portfolio companies we can reference
[Companies in their portfolio that are customers, partners, or in adjacent space]

### Our specific thesis fit angle
[In 2 sentences: exactly why this deal fits their stated thesis — be specific, not generic.
"We fit your B2B SaaS thesis" is not specific. "Our 8-month CAC payback and 92% gross
margin put CloudMed in the top quartile of health SaaS metrics your portfolio targets at
Seed" is specific.]

### Portfolio conflicts
[None identified / [Conflict: Company X is in portfolio — approach only if confirmed
non-compete market segment]]

### Known deal terms (if available)
[Check sizes from Crunchbase / portfolio company disclosures — estimate check size range]

### Questions to ask them (3 minimum — shows research)
1. [Question about a recent portfolio investment that relates to your thesis]
2. [Question about their fund strategy for LATAM — shows you know their context]
3. [Question about what would make this a "yes" for them — invites direct feedback]

### Warm intro path
[Name of person who made the intro / "Cold"]

### What success looks like
[Specific next step after this meeting: second meeting, data room request, term sheet discussion]
```

## Red Flags in Investor Research

**Investor is not actively deploying:**
- No new investments in 12+ months → fund may be in harvest mode
- Fund is raising their next vintage → attention is on LP roadshows, not new investments
- **Action:** Ask directly: "Is [Fund Name] actively deploying from your current fund?"

**Thesis mismatch:**
- Fund claims to invest in your sector but all portfolio companies are 3× larger than you
  → they may be stage-misrepresenting themselves to see more deal flow
- Fund has no LATAM portfolio companies despite claiming LATAM focus
  → question whether they actually close in LATAM or just say they do
- **Action:** Filter these from your primary list; route to secondary "if we need to fill the round" list

**Portfolio conflict found:**
- Direct competitor in portfolio → remove from list entirely
- Adjacent company in portfolio → approach with extreme caution; confirm non-compete market segment
- **Action:** Never approach a fund with a direct portfolio conflict. It wastes both parties' time.

## LATAM-Specific Research Notes

**LAVCA Membership:** LAVCA (Latin America Venture Capital Association) member funds are
typically more institutionally governed. LAVCA publishes annual reports with fund-by-fund
data — useful for identifying active deployers.
Source: https://www.lavca.org

**Decile Hub LATAM data:** Decile Hub aggregates LATAM fund performance data with
GP-level granularity. Useful for understanding a fund's historical performance.
Source: https://www.decilehub.com

**Family office funds:** Many LATAM "VC funds" are family office vehicles with 2–5
investments per year and no formal LP structure. Research whether the fund has external LPs
(institutional or other family offices) — this affects governance, follow-on capacity,
and deal speed. If it is a single-family-office vehicle, expect: slower decisions,
more relationship time required, simpler term sheets.
