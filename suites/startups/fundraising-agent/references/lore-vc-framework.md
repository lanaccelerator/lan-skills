## Overview

The lore.vc framework structures venture fundraising as a 3-phase process, not a
single event. Each phase has specific objectives, timelines, and tactics. The most
common founder mistake is treating fundraising as one continuous effort rather than
three distinct activities with different goals, materials, and investor psychology.

---

## Phase A: Preparation (4–6 weeks)

**Objective:** Be ready before approaching a single investor.

The lore.vc framework is emphatic on this point: founders who approach investors
before materials are complete waste their most valuable asset — the first impression.
Once an investor passes, re-approaching requires a much higher bar. You only get
one first meeting per investor per round.

### What must be complete before Phase B:

**1. VC-backability confirmation**
The fund return math must work. At a $2M Seed: the lead investor needs to believe
the company can be worth $200M+ in 7–10 years (10× their check) and that the probability
of that outcome is high enough to justify the investment vs. alternatives.
If VC-backability is unclear — run /venture-model-validator before proceeding.

**2. Stage-appropriate metrics**
Investors evaluate you against stage benchmarks, not your own trajectory.
- Pre-seed: concept + founding team credentials
- Seed: $50–150k MRR or equivalent leading indicator + proven demand signal
- Series A: $1–3M ARR + consistent growth + unit economics visibility
If metrics are below stage benchmarks — fix them before fundraising. Raising before
metrics are ready is raising at a lower valuation than you deserve.

**3. Pitch deck (complete, reviewed)**
The deck tells the story. The data room provides the evidence. You need both.
A deck without a data room loses trust in diligence. A data room without a deck
loses the investor's attention before they review anything.

**4. Data room (assembled, not just outlined)**
Critical documents: cap table (certified), financial model (3-year), customer contracts
(2–3 samples), team bios, IP assignment agreements (founders), 3 months bank statements.
The #1 LATAM Seed diligence failure: IP assignment agreements not in place.

**5. Investor target list (30 names, 9-column format)**
Quality over quantity. 30 well-researched investors with warm intro paths beats 100
cold emails with a 2% response rate.

**6. Warm intro mapping**
For each investor: identify the specific person who can make the intro, and request
that intro before approaching the investor directly. Double opt-in intros are the
gold standard — you provide the connector a 3-sentence summary and deck to forward.

### LATAM Preparation Adaptation:

- Start Phase A 3 months earlier than US-equivalent timeline. LATAM investors have
  longer evaluation cycles (4–6 weeks vs. 2–3 weeks in US).
- Build 15-month runway before starting Phase A. Not 12 months. Fundraising will
  take 3 months longer than you expect.
- Build your warm intro map before your target list. In LATAM VC, cold outreach has
  near-zero conversion. If you have no warm intro path to an investor, they are
  effectively not on your list.

## Phase B: Fundraising (8–12 weeks)

**Objective:** Run a parallel process that creates genuine urgency through competition.

The single most important tactical principle from lore.vc: investors move faster when
they believe other serious investors are close to committing. This is not deception —
it is creating a parallel process where multiple investors evaluate simultaneously,
and communicating that process honestly.

### Parallel process mechanics:

1. **Launch cohort:** Contact all target investors in a 1-week window. Do not drip
   outreach over months. The goal is simultaneous first meetings within the same
   2-week period.
2. **Honest status updates:** When investor A asks "are other investors interested?",
   the honest answer should be "yes, we are running a focused process with 8–10 funds
   and expect to make a decision within 6 weeks." This is not pressure — it is
   professional process management.
3. **Signal amplification:** One signed term sheet creates urgency for all other open
   conversations. Use this honestly: "We have a term sheet from [Fund X], we have
   10 days to respond, and we would prefer to complete the round with you as co-lead
   or participant. Can we accelerate our conversation?"

### Outreach sequence:

1. Warm intros first (all paths exhausted before cold)
2. Cold email (use templates in references/outreach-templates.md) — only after warm
   intro channels are exhausted for that specific investor
3. Follow-up cadence: Day 1 / Day 7 / Day 14 / Day 21 breakup

### Meeting prep:

For every confirmed meeting, produce a per-investor prep brief:
- Investor's thesis and recent investments (last 6 months)
- Portfolio companies you share or can reference as customers/partners
- Specific thesis fit angle (why this deal fits their thesis specifically — not generically)
- Questions to ask the investor (shows research, builds relationship)
See references/investor-research-guide.md for full prep methodology.

### Process management:

Track every investor in a spreadsheet: Stage | Last Contact | Next Step | Deadline.
Fundraising without a tracker loses opportunities to follow-up at the right moment.
The #1 mistake in Phase B: irregular follow-up that loses momentum.

### LATAM Phase B Adaptation:

- Cold conversion: near zero. If you have not exhausted warm intro paths, do not
  send cold outreach yet.
- Response timeline: LATAM investors take 2–3 weeks to respond to first outreach vs.
  1 week in US. Build this into your timeline.
- Meeting frequency: Many LATAM funds meet founders 3+ times before committing vs.
  2 meetings in US. Budget for longer evaluation.
- Family office LPs in LATAM VC funds: evaluation is personal and relationship-based.
  Founders who meet the LP directly (not just the GP) close faster.

## Phase C: Closing (2–4 weeks)

**Objective:** Convert term sheets into signed docs and wired capital.

The lore.vc framework's closing insight: the period between term sheet and close
is the highest-risk phase of the fundraise, not the fundraising itself. More rounds
fall apart in diligence than in outreach.

### Term sheet receipt:

Read every term carefully against shared/brad-feld-terms.md.
The three terms that founders most often regret signing:
1. Participating preferred (takes money twice in acquisitions)
2. Investor board majority before Series B (loses strategic control)
3. Full ratchet anti-dilution (catastrophic in down round)

Do not rush closing because you are excited to have a term sheet. A bad term sheet
is worse than no term sheet — you will live with these terms for 7–10 years.

### Due diligence management:

Organize all DD requests through your data room. Do not send documents via email
without version tracking. Use a DD request tracker (see TEMPLATES.md).

Timeline expectations:
- Seed DD: 3–6 weeks typical
- Series A DD: 6–12 weeks typical

The most common DD failure in LATAM: founders cannot produce IP assignment agreements
(showing that founders legally transferred their pre-company IP to the company).
If this is missing, fix it in Phase A, not Phase C.

### Legal close:

At minimum, a startup lawyer must review:
- Stock purchase agreement
- Investor rights agreement
- Voting agreement
- Right of first refusal and co-sale agreement (ROFR/Co-Sale)

Do not use a lawyer who has not worked on LATAM VC deals before. Term sheets from
US investors in Mexico or Colombia require lawyers who understand both jurisdictions.

### Post-close:

Within 48 hours: announce to advisors and board. Prepare investor update cadence
(monthly for Seed, quarterly for Series A). Start sending monthly updates immediately —
investors who receive consistent updates are 3× more likely to lead the next round.

### LATAM Phase C Adaptation:

- Wire timing: LATAM banks require 3–7 business days for international wires.
  Build this into your close timeline.
- Currency: Wires from US investors to Colombian/Mexican entities require FX conversion
  planning. Work with your bank on the FX rate risk during the closing window.
- Legal structure: Colombian SAS, Mexican SAPI, or Delaware C-Corp (for US institutional
  investors). Confirm legal structure is investable before the term sheet is signed.

## Key Principles (lore.vc framework summary)

1. **Prepare before approaching.** One first impression per investor per round.
2. **Run a parallel process.** Sequential approaches are slow and signal low demand.
3. **Warm intros dominate.** Cold outreach converts at 1–3%. Warm intros convert at 25–40%.
4. **Track everything.** Fundraising is a sales process. Run it like one.
5. **Closing is the highest-risk phase.** Most rounds fail in diligence, not outreach.
6. **Follow up consistently.** Investors are busy. Your round is not their priority.
7. **LATAM adds 3 months.** Build this into your timeline from Day 1 of Phase A.
