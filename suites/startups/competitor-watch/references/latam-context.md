# LATAM Context: Watching Competitors from Latin America

**Source:** LAN Accelerator operator read from LATAM B2B founders (2023-2026), directional.
For benchmark numbers, see shared/latam-benchmarks.md.
**When to use:** competitor-watch Steps 2-5, to pick the right pages and read the signals
when the startup sells in Latin America or competes with US companies entering the region.

---

## 1. The default watch list: 5 US competitors plus the baseline

Most LATAM B2B startups face two kinds of competitors: local players and US companies that
are, or will be, entering. The default watch list favors US competitors because they publish
more (pricing pages, changelogs, careers boards) and because their LATAM entry is the event
that changes a local startup's pitch and pricing. Add local competitors when the founder
names them, with the adjusted sources below.

## 2. Signals that a US competitor is entering LATAM

Usually visible weeks or months before any press release:

| Signal | Where |
|---|---|
| Spanish or Portuguese version of the site or the pricing page | Language switcher, `/es`, `/pt-br` paths |
| Prices in MXN, BRL, COP, CLP or PYG, or "precios en tu moneda" | Pricing page |
| Local payment methods: PIX, OXXO, Boleto, Mercado Pago, local cards in installments | Pricing or checkout pages |
| Roles in São Paulo, Mexico City, Bogotá, Santiago, Buenos Aires, or "LATAM" in the title | Careers page, LinkedIn jobs, Greenhouse or Lever boards |
| Local compliance features: CFDI (Mexico), NF-e and e-Social (Brazil), electronic invoicing (Chile, Colombia, Paraguay) | Changelog, docs |
| LATAM case studies, a regional partner or reseller, sponsorship of a regional event | Blog, news page |

One signal is a hypothesis. Two in the same quarter is a plan. Flag it as material.

## 3. Local competitors leave different traces

- **Pricing is rarely public.** "Cotizá por WhatsApp" or "agendá una demo" is common. Record
  it as the value and watch for the day a price appears. Ask new customers what they were
  quoted (with consent) and file it as INFERRED.
- **Changelogs are rare.** Watch the product's social posts, the help center and app store
  release notes (Google Play and App Store show version history publicly).
- **Job posts live on regional boards:** LinkedIn, Computrabajo, Bumeran, Get on Board (tech
  roles across the region), Indeed, InfoJobs (Brazil), Laborum (Chile). Use public listings or
  web search results.
- **Funding and corporate events** show up first in regional media and registries: LAVCA
  Venture Bulletin, Contxto, LatamList, Bloomberg Línea, and public company registries (CNPJ in
  Brazil, RUT in Chile, RUC in Paraguay).
- **WhatsApp communities and gremio events** are where local competitors announce things to
  their customers. A founder who is a member can paste what was shared publicly in the group;
  never forward private messages.

## 4. The Claude/ChatGPT baseline in LATAM

Many SME owners and managers in the region already use the free or about US$20 tier of
ChatGPT or Claude from their phone, often for writing, translation and spreadsheets. Watch the
assistants' release notes for features that close the gaps local startups rely on: Spanish and
Portuguese quality, local file formats (CFDI XML, NF-e), WhatsApp integration and connectors
to local ERPs or accounting tools.

## 5. Reading changes with LATAM eyes

- **A US price cut is not always a threat.** If the competitor still has no local invoicing,
  no local payment methods and no Spanish or Portuguese support, the price only matters to
  buyers who already pay in US dollars by card.
- **Currency moves.** A competitor billing in US dollars gets more expensive for local buyers
  when the local currency weakens; that is an opening for a startup billing in local currency.
  Note the exchange rate on the day of the snapshot when comparing prices across currencies.
- **Hiring in the region beats announcements.** A LATAM account executive posted this month
  matters more than a "global expansion" blog post.
