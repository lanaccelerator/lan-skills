# Daily Review Framework

Why a written daily close and a light morning agenda work, and the rules
that keep them useful. Read before writing a brief.

## The practice behind it

- **Newport, Deep Work (2016), "Rule #1: Work Deeply", shutdown ritual:** at
  the end of the workday, review every open task and commitment, make sure
  each is captured with a plan, check tomorrow's calendar, then end the day
  explicitly. Newport cites Zeigarnik-effect research (Masicampo and
  Baumeister, 2011): unfinished tasks keep intruding on attention, but
  writing a concrete plan for them is enough to release that pull. The
  close mode is this ritual, automated.
- **Grove, High Output Management (1983), information gathering:** a
  manager's output depends on timely, condensed information. Grove values
  reports less for being read than for forcing the writer to organize the
  facts, and prefers short regular inputs over long irregular ones. The
  team mode is a condensed daily information feed for cofounders.
- **Allen, Getting Things Done (2001), open loops and review:** anything
  committed but not captured is an open loop that consumes attention. A
  pendiente is only closed when it is written as a next action with a
  context. The weekly review is GTD's anchor; the daily close is the
  lightweight version that keeps the weekly one short.
- **Morning planning, not morning reading:** the open mode is deliberately
  light. It gives the agenda and the carried-over list, and points to the
  single most important item. It does not re-summarize yesterday.

## What makes a line belong in the brief

| Keep | Drop |
|---|---|
| A decision made or received | Newsletters, notifications, automated mail |
| A commitment by or to the user (verb plus date) | Threads where the user was only CC'd and nothing changed |
| Money: price, invoice, payment, term sheet | Calendar holds with no attendees and no notes |
| A customer, investor or hire moving a stage | Every commit SHA (group by repo and purpose) |
| Something shipped (commit, deploy, document sent) | AI sessions with no lasting output |
| A blocker or risk that changes tomorrow | Speculation about what someone "probably" meant |

## Pendientes rules

- Written as a next action: verb, deliverable, date when known.
- Split "tuyos abiertos" (the user owes) from "esperando a otros" (owed to
  the user, with who and since when).
- Carry-over counter: an item copied from yesterday increases its day count.
  At 3 days, the brief asks: do it first, delegate, or drop.
- A pendiente leaves the list only with evidence it was done (a sent email,
  a commit, a note) or an explicit drop by the user.

## Source discipline

- Every line ends with a source tag. No tag, no line.
- Today means the user's local calendar day, 00:00 to the run time.
- If two sources disagree (calendar says a meeting happened, the notes say it
  was cancelled), the later-written source wins and the conflict goes under
  "Pendientes de verificar".
- Inferences are allowed only when labeled `[inferido]` and never in team
  mode.

## Team mode boundaries

- Built from shared sources only: brain pages, shared calendars, CRM
  activity, commits in shared repos.
- Never reads a teammate's private email, DMs or personal calendar details.
- Excludes compensation, performance evaluations, legal, health and
  personal matters even when they appear in shared sources.
- Describes work, not people: "Ventas: 3 demos, 1 propuesta enviada", not
  judgments about effort.
