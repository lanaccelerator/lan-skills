## BCG Corporate Venturing Framework — 4 CVC Models

**Source:** https://www.bcg.com/publications/2021/corporate-venture-capital

BCG identifies four structural models for corporate venture activity. Each has distinct capital requirements, organizational design, and strategic outcomes:

### Model 1: Captive CVC Fund

**Structure:** Corporate owns 100% of the fund. Internal team manages all investments.
**Organizational requirement:** Dedicated CVC team (minimum 2-3 investment professionals) with prior VC experience. Compensation must be at or near market VC rates — or talent will not be attracted or retained.
**Best for:** Companies with >$1B revenue, dedicated investment team, and 7-10 year time horizon.
**Risks:**
- Talent retention: corporate salary structures cannot compete with carry-based VC compensation
- Governance conflict: corporate parent may override investment decisions for strategic reasons, destroying financial return potential
- Speed: captive CVC IC approval cycles average 3-6 months vs. 4-6 weeks for independent VCs

**Minimum viable setup (BCG):**
- Committed capital: $50M+ (below this, portfolio diversification is insufficient)
- Investment team: 2-3 senior investment professionals with prior VC experience
- IC composition: CVC Director + CFO + CEO representative + 1 external VC observer

### Model 2: LP in External Fund

**Structure:** Corporate commits capital to an independent VC fund as a limited partner.
**Organizational requirement:** 1 strategic partnership manager (not a full investment team). Commitment management, not investment management.
**Best for:** Corporations with $200M-$1B revenue, or early-stage CVC programs testing the model before building internal capability.
**Advantages:**
- Access to deal flow and portfolio companies via the fund's network (co-investment rights typically included)
- No talent competition — the fund manager handles HR, compensation, and team retention
- Financial returns benchmarked to the fund's overall performance, not just the corporate's picks
**Disadvantages:**
- Limited strategic control — fund manager has fiduciary duty to all LPs, not just the corporate
- Co-investment rights are not guarantees — quality deals go to LPs with strategic value to the startup
- Information rights are limited — corporate cannot direct startups in the portfolio

**Minimum viable LP commitment:** $5-15M (below $5M, the corporate lacks strategic LP influence)

### Model 3: Accelerator Partnership

**Structure:** Corporate sponsors a cohort within an established accelerator program (Wayra, Rockstart, 500 Global LATAM, Platanus, Parallel18). May include optional investment rights.
**Best for:** Corporations with <$500M revenue, or those in early exploration stage without CVC governance infrastructure.
**Investment:** $500k-$2M per cohort cycle (sponsorship fees + optional investments)
**Advantages:**
- Immediate access to startup pipeline without building a CVC
- Talent for accelerator management is provided by the accelerator operator
- Option to invest (typically SAFE at accelerator valuation cap) without lead investor obligations
**Disadvantages:**
- No proprietary deal flow — all accelerator corporate partners see the same cohort
- Startups are earlier-stage → longer time to any financial return
- Strategic control is minimal

### Model 4: Balance Sheet Investment

**Structure:** Ad-hoc corporate development team makes direct investments from corporate balance sheet, no formal CVC structure.
**Best for:** Opportunistic strategic investments where a specific startup relationship requires equity stake.
**Risk:** High coordination cost, no portfolio strategy, not scalable. Each investment requires full corporate board approval. This model produces 1-3 investments per year maximum.

## Intel Capital Case Study — Strategic Thesis Discipline

**Source:** Intel Capital LATAM strategy overview (2008-2022)

Intel Capital's approach in LATAM demonstrated that technology CVCs with a clear, defensible strategic thesis significantly outperform generalist corporate venture programs.

**Intel Capital's thesis discipline:**
- Every investment must serve Intel's core business (semiconductor, platform, ecosystem)
- Adjacency exploration ("this could be interesting") was explicitly excluded from the investment mandate
- Portfolio companies received real strategic value (supply chain access, co-marketing, developer ecosystem) — not just capital

**Key LATAM lesson:** Intel Capital's LATAM investments in Brazil, Mexico, and Colombia (2008-2020) showed that portfolio companies receiving strategic support (supply chain access, co-sell arrangements) had higher survival rates than companies receiving capital only (directional, source unverified).

**Implication for LATAM CVC design:** Define the strategic support the corporate can provide (customers, distribution, co-sell, technology access) before the investment thesis. If the answer is "access to capital," the CVC has no competitive advantage over independent VCs.

## Corporate Governance Design for CVC Programs

**Investment Committee (IC) composition — minimum viable design:**

| Role | Requirement | Why |
|------|-------------|-----|
| CVC Director | VC experience required (not M&A) | Sets evaluation standards, manages partner relationships |
| CFO or delegate | Senior financial officer | Financial return accountability, capital allocation authority |
| CEO or delegate | C-suite authority | Strategic alignment decisions, portfolio company access |
| External VC observer | Non-voting but present | Calibrates IC decisions to market standards; prevents corporate M&A patterns |

**IC decision rights:**
- Investments ≤ $2M: CVC Director + CFO approval (no full IC required)
- Investments $2M-$10M: Full IC approval
- Investments > $10M: Board-level approval required
- Follow-on investments: CVC Director authority up to pro-rata rights; above pro-rata = IC approval

**Legal review cost caps (critical for CVC economics):**
- Investments ≤ $2M: Legal review capped at $15-25k (SAFE or standard term sheet, no custom legal)
- Investments $2M-$10M: Legal review capped at $50-75k
- Investments > $10M: Standard M&A legal review applies
- CVC programs that apply full M&A legal review to all investments become economically impossible below $5M check sizes — average LATAM CVC legal review with corporate M&A standards: $150-200k per deal
