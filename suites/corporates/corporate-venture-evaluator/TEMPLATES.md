## Readiness Classification

**READY WITH CONDITIONS** — Grupo Sura has strong strategic fit and sufficient committed capital for a meaningful CVC program. Two conditions must be resolved before first check:

1. **Financial return horizon extended to 8 years minimum** (board approval required)
2. **External VC observer added to IC** (non-voting; must have early-stage VC investment experience)

Without these two conditions, Sura's CVC program will be evaluated with M&A standards on a 5-year horizon — structurally incompatible with early-stage venture returns.

---

## Innovation Theater Indicators

**[THEATER] $180k Legal Review Per Investment:**
Diego's proposal routes all CVC investments through Sura's standard M&A legal review process —
the same process used for corporate acquisitions. At $180k per deal, a $1M insurtech SAFE investment
costs Sura 18% of the check in legal fees alone. This makes the program economically impossible for
investments below $5M. Sura's full $30M could theoretically fund 30+ investments at $1M average —
but would generate $5.4M in legal fees before any startup delivers value. This is not a CVC. It is
an M&A program with startup branding.

**Resolution:** Board must approve a separate "CVC Legal Framework" capping legal review at $20k for
SAFE investments ≤ $2M, and $50k for priced rounds ≤ $5M. This is standard practice at TOTVS Ventures
and Grupo Bancolombia Innovation Fund.

**[THEATER] Colombia-Only Deal Flow Constraint:**
Colombia's insurtech ecosystem has ~15-20 active companies/year at seed/series A. A $30M fund
investing $1-2M checks needs 15-30 investments for minimum diversification. At Colombia-only pace,
full deployment takes 10-15 years — incompatible with a 7-year fund life. Sura must expand deal flow
access to Brazil (largest LATAM insurtech market: 80+ companies) and Mexico (30+ companies).

**Resolution:** LP commitment of $10-15M to a LATAM-focused insurtech/fintech VC (Kaszek, Canary,
or ACE Ventures) provides immediate deal flow access across LATAM while Sura builds direct
Colombian deal flow.

## Optimal CVC Model Recommendation

**Recommended Model: Hybrid — LP + Captive CVC**

Given 14/20 readiness with two conditions, Sura should NOT launch a full captive CVC immediately.
The recommended path:

**Phase 1 (Year 1-2): LP Commitment ($15M)**
Commit $15M to a LATAM-focused insurtech/fintech VC fund as a strategic LP with co-investment rights.
Target funds: Kaszek (Brazil/LATAM), Canary (fintech-focused), or ACE Ventures (Colombia/LATAM).
- This provides immediate deal flow access across LATAM
- Co-investment rights allow Sura to deploy the remaining $15M in companies it wants direct equity in
- Sura builds investment pattern recognition without managing a portfolio from day one

**Phase 2 (Year 2-4): Build Direct CVC Capability ($15M)**
While the LP commitment matures:
- Hire CVC Director (must have VC experience — not Diego's M&A background)
- Add external VC observer to IC
- Build direct investment pipeline through Ruta N and Platanus Colombia relationships
- Deploy $15M in 10-15 direct investments using co-investment rights from Phase 1 fund

**Phase 3 (Year 5-7): Full Captive CVC Assessment**
At Year 5 with portfolio data, Sura has evidence for the board to evaluate whether a standalone
captive CVC (for Fund II) is warranted or whether the LP + co-investment model is the permanent structure.

## Next Steps

1. **Secure board approval for 8-year CVC horizon by Q2 2026.** Diego must present the
   "strategic cost of not investing" framing to the board. The question is not "what is the
   financial IRR?" — it is "what does it cost Sura if the next Nubank or next insurtech
   disruptor builds in Latin America without Sura's involvement?" This framing converts a
   financial return discussion into a strategic risk discussion.

2. **Approve a CVC-specific legal framework capping legal review at $20k for SAFE investments
   ≤ $2M.** This is a prerequisite for any CVC activity. Without it, Sura cannot compete
   for quality deals. Present the Grupo Bancolombia Innovation Fund legal framework as a
   template — they resolved this exact problem in 2022.

3. **Hire a CVC Director with VC investment experience before deploying any capital.**
   Requirements: (a) 5+ years as VC partner or principal, (b) led at least 3 investments
   as lead investor, (c) existing LATAM insurtech/fintech relationships. An M&A banker or
   investment banker with no VC experience does not qualify, regardless of financial
   sophistication.

4. **Execute LP commitment of $15M to one LATAM-focused fund within 12 months.**
   Target: Kaszek (Brazil/LATAM), Canary (fintech-focused), or ACE Ventures (Colombia/LATAM).
   Negotiate co-investment rights explicitly in LP agreement. Without co-investment rights,
   the LP commitment is passive capital — not a CVC strategy.

5. **Add external VC observer to IC before first direct investment.** Non-voting, but present
   for all IC decisions. Role: calibrate Sura's IC discussions to market standards and prevent
   M&A evaluation patterns from being applied to startup investments. Partner-level at a LATAM
   VC fund is the right profile.
