## Procurement Timelines

| Scenario | Timeline | Driver |
|----------|----------|--------|
| LATAM-native startup with LATAM entity | 6-8 weeks to signed contract | Standard corporate MSA accepted |
| US startup with LATAM subsidiary | 4-6 months | US tax withholding setup, subsidiary verification, local compliance |
| US startup, no LATAM entity | 5-9 months | US parent contract, withholding tax (15-25%), legal review of cross-border services |
| SME startup, no legal team | 4-9 months | Startup cannot negotiate corporate MSA → requires template contract provided by corporate |

**Practical implication for shortlisting:**
- If the pilot timeline is 6 months, ONLY LATAM-native startups are procurement-feasible.
- If the pilot timeline is 12+ months, US startups with subsidiary structures are feasible.
- "Global leader with no LATAM entity" is a procurement trap — evaluated, approved, then killed at legal review.

## Fiscal Invoice Compliance by Country

A common but preventable pilot failure: the startup cannot issue a compliant fiscal invoice
in the corporate's country. This blocks payment processing, triggers corporate accounts
payable rejection, and kills the contract at the final step.

| Country | Invoice Standard | Startup Failure Rate | What to Verify |
|---------|-----------------|---------------------|----------------|
| Brazil | NF-e (Nota Fiscal Eletrônica) | High | Can startup issue NF-e from a Brazilian CNPJ? |
| Mexico | CFDI (Comprobante Fiscal Digital por Internet) | High | Can startup issue CFDI via an RFC (taxpayer ID)? |
| Colombia | e-invoicing (DIAN compliant) | Medium | Does startup have Colombia legal entity with DIAN registration? |
| Argentina | AFIP electronic invoice | High | FX complications; peso invoice vs USD contract creates accounting discrepancy |
| Chile | SII e-invoicing | Low | Most enterprise-ready LATAM startups can issue SII invoices |

**Rule:** If a startup cannot issue a compliant fiscal invoice in the corporate's primary market,
remove them from the shortlist. This is not an edge case — it is the #3 reason LATAM pilots fail
(directional, source unverified).

## Pilot Budget Calibration

**Source:** directional, source unverified

| Pilot Scope | LATAM Budget Range | US Equivalent |
|-------------|-------------------|--------------|
| Proof of concept (1-2 systems, 30-day test) | $20-50k USD | $50-100k |
| Minimum viable pilot (1 department, 90 days) | $50-100k USD | $150-300k |
| Full enterprise pilot (cross-department, 6 months) | $100-250k USD | $300-600k |
| Production rollout (post-pilot scale) | $250k-1M+ USD | $500k-3M+ |

**Warning:** $30-50k is typically consumed by procurement overhead (legal review, IT security
assessment, vendor onboarding) before the pilot produces any data. Do not shortlist pilots
with total budgets under $50k unless the startup has an existing MSA with the corporate.

## Top Corporate-Startup Procurement Failures in LATAM

**Source:** Plug and Play LATAM Corporate Innovation Report 2024

Ranked by frequency:

1. **No internal champion** (38%) — Committee-driven pilots have no decision-maker. First roadblock kills the initiative.
2. **Integration complexity exceeded IT capacity** (29%) — IT team committed but could not deliver bandwidth. Pilot stalled.
3. **Fiscal invoice compliance failure** (18%) — Startup could not issue compliant invoice in corporate's country. AP team rejected payment.
4. **Budget consumed by procurement overhead** (15%) — Legal review, security assessment, and vendor onboarding exceeded pilot budget.
5. **Startup not LATAM-entity-able before contract deadline** (12%) — Startup agreed to create LATAM entity but could not complete in time. Corporate moved on.

**Structural implication for shortlisting:** Weight LATAM entity and fiscal compliance heavily (these are binary filters, not scoring criteria). The startups that clear these filters are the actual addressable shortlist.
