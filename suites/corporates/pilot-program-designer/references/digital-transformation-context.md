# Digital Transformation Context — Corporate Pilot Design Methodology

**Source:** Digital Transformation 2025 — Corporate-Startup Pilot Programs chapter.
**When to use:** This file is read by pilot-program-designer during Step 2 (framework application)
and Step 3 (workflow). Contains Techstars, Wayra, and 500 Global pilot structure methodology
plus LATAM pilot failure statistics.

## Techstars Corporate Innovation Pilot Model

**Source:** https://www.techstars.com/accelerators/corporate-innovation

**Structure:** Sprint-based, 13-week program with biweekly check-ins and 3 formal go/no-go gates.

**4 success criteria types (apply all four to every pilot design):**
1. **Technical** — Does it work? Integration completed, no critical errors in production data
2. **Commercial** — Is there ROI? Cost reduction or revenue lift measurable above baseline
3. **Organizational** — Will teams use it? Adoption rate meets threshold, not abandoned after week 3
4. **Strategic** — Does it advance our thesis? Pilot outcome informs a future strategic decision

**Kill rate insight:** Techstars corporate programs kill approximately 40% of pilots at the
6-week gate. This is healthy, not failure. A 40% kill rate at the midpoint gate means the
program is functioning correctly — it identified non-viable directions early and redirected
resources. A 0% kill rate means criteria were not enforced.

**What Techstars requires before pilot start:**
- Named executive sponsor (VP level or above)
- Written success criteria with numeric thresholds
- IT point of contact allocated for duration
- Legal framework (MSA or pilot agreement) signed before work begins

## Wayra Pilot Framework (LATAM-Specific)

**Source:** https://wayra.com/corporate-innovation

**Structure:** 90-day proof of concept, 3-phase model:
- Days 1–30: Integration setup — API access, test environment, data ingestion, legal/compliance
- Days 31–60: Live pilot with real data — limited user group, weekly performance metrics
- Days 61–90: Evaluation and commercial negotiation — kill criteria applied, GO/NO-GO decision

**Wayra LATAM success metric:** Approximately 25% of pilots reach commercial contract within
12 months of pilot start. This is the LATAM benchmark — not a high bar. Top-quartile corporate
innovation programs achieve 35–40% pilot-to-commercial conversion.

**Wayra kill criteria requirement:** Wayra mandates written kill criteria before pilot approval.
Programs submitted without kill criteria are rejected from the portfolio. The rationale: if the
corporate cannot define what failure looks like, the pilot will never reach a decision.

**Wayra budget guidance (LATAM):**
- Minimum viable pilot budget: $60k USD (startup fee + IT + legal + contingency)
- Recommended range: $80–150k USD for meaningful 90-day POC
- Under $50k: insufficient for legal + IT + startup onboarding in LATAM enterprise context

## 500 Global Corporate Programs

**Source:** https://500.co/corporations

**Structure:** 4-stage model with decision points at each stage:
1. **Scouting** — Corporate defines problem statement + evaluation criteria
2. **Pilot** — 60–120 day POC with defined KPIs and kill criteria
3. **Commercial** — Negotiation of commercial terms based on pilot results
4. **Scale** — Deployment across business units / geographies

**Average pilot → commercial conversion:** 18% (vs. 35% in top-quartile corporates globally).
The gap between 18% and 35% is driven by kill criteria enforcement: top-quartile programs
terminate low-performers at the midpoint gate and redeploy resources to higher-potential pilots.
Programs that do not enforce kill criteria maintain zombie pilots that suppress the average.

**500 Global pilot design requirements:**
- Problem statement: metric-based (current → target), not qualitative
- KPIs: 3–5 maximum (more than 5 creates measurement noise)
- Decision gate: at 60–70% of pilot duration, not only at the end
- Kill criteria: both success and failure thresholds, both required

## LATAM Pilot Failure Statistics

**Source:** BCG LATAM Corporate Innovation Benchmarking 2024

**Top 3 failure causes (65% of LATAM pilot failures):**
1. No internal champion identified (28% of failures) — pilot deprioritized when IT bandwidth
   competed with internal projects; no one to escalate or protect pilot resources
2. Unclear success criteria (22% of failures) — no numeric threshold, evaluation became
   subjective; corporate team "not convinced" without defined bar to convince them
3. IT integration failure (15% of failures) — IT not allocated to pilot, enterprise systems
   access not secured before pilot start, integration took 60% of pilot duration

**Average zombie pilot lifetime:** 14 months consuming $180k in internal resources before
termination (BCG LATAM 2024). A zombie pilot is one that has no kill criteria — it runs
until someone senior enough gets frustrated and terminates it, by which point the startup
has burned runway waiting for a decision.

**LATAM vs. global pilot-to-commercial conversion rates:**
- Global average: 22%
- LATAM average: 18%
- LATAM top-quartile (programs with kill criteria enforced): 33%
- LATAM bottom-quartile (no kill criteria): 8%

**Key insight:** The 25 percentage point gap between top-quartile and bottom-quartile LATAM
corporate programs is explained almost entirely by kill criteria enforcement. Kill criteria
are not bureaucratic process — they are the mechanism that forces a decision and prevents
zombie accumulation.

## 5-Component Pilot Design Framework

Apply in every pilot design:

| Component | Purpose | Common Failure |
|-----------|---------|----------------|
| 1. SMART Problem Statement | Aligns everyone on what success means before it happens | Vague problem = moving goalposts |
| 2. KPI Dashboard (3–5 KPIs) | Lead indicators show trajectory; lag indicators drive GO/NO-GO | Too many KPIs = no clear signal |
| 3. Timeline with gates | Two-thirds gate forces a midpoint decision, not just an end-of-pilot review | No midpoint gate = zombies survive to end |
| 4. Budget allocation | Explicit IT + legal budget prevents resource starvation | Startup fee only = pilot stalls at integration |
| 5. Kill Criteria | The GO/NO-GO mechanism — without it, the pilot has no endpoint | Missing = zombie |
