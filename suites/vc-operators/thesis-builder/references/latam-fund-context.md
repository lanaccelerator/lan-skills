## LATAM Structural Shifts for VC Thesis Building

### Structural Enablers (2020–2026)

**Payment infrastructure revolution:**
- PIX (Brazil, launched Nov 2020): 150M+ active accounts, 60B+ annual transactions.
  Creates real-time cash flow data for SME underwriting. Source: Banco Central do Brasil 2025.
- Open Banking Mandate (Brazil Phase 4 complete; Mexico Phase 3 live 2025): CNBV requires
  all financial institutions to share transactional data with authorized fintechs via API.
  Source: CNBV Mexico 2025.
- CFDI 4.0 Digital Invoice Mandate (Mexico, Jan 2024): 100% of SMEs with >$200k annual
  revenue required to issue digital invoices via SAT. Creates real-time revenue verification.
  Source: SAT Mexico 2024.
- CoDi (Mexico, 2019) and SPEI real-time payments: Mexico's interbank transfer
  infrastructure created the plumbing for digital payments to SME customers at zero cost.

**Smartphone and internet penetration:**
- Brazil: 87% smartphone penetration, 84% internet penetration (IBGE 2025)
- Mexico: 82% smartphone penetration, 79% internet penetration (INEGI 2025)
- LATAM average: 85% smartphone penetration for working-age population

### LATAM VC Market Data

**Source:** Cuantico 2026 LATAM VC Report: https://reports.cuanticovp.com/latin-america-vc-report-2026/
**Source:** LAVCA 2025 Startup Ecosystem Insights: https://www.lavca.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/LAVCA_Startup-Ecosystem-Insights_2025.pdf

**Total LATAM VC invested 2025:**
- $4.126B across 681 rounds `(LATAM, Cuantico 2026)`
- Q4 2025: $1.2B — strongest quarter since Q4 2022
- Deal count declining (quality > quantity dynamic in post-2022 correction)

**Sector breakdown (2025):**
- Fintech: 61% of all LATAM VC investment `(LATAM, Cuantico 2026)`
- SaaS / Enterprise Software: 13%
- Healthtech: 9%
- Logistics / Supply Chain: 8%
- Edtech: 4%
- Other: 5%

**Implication for fintech theses:** 61% concentration means fintech thesis funds
compete against 40+ LATAM-focused fintech funds. A generic "fintech in LATAM" thesis
does not differentiate. Sub-sector specificity is required: SME credit, insurance,
wealth management, payroll, payments infrastructure, or regulatory tech.

**Implication for SaaS theses:** 13% SaaS allocation vs. 30%+ in US indicates either
an opportunity (underallocation) or a structural limit (LATAM buyers prefer local,
relationship-based vendors; procurement cycles 2–3× longer than US). A SaaS thesis
must address this gap explicitly.

**Geography breakdown (2025):**
- Brazil: 47% of total LATAM VC capital `(LATAM, Cuantico 2026)`
- Mexico: 31.5%
- Combined Brazil + Mexico: 78.5%
- Colombia: 8%, Argentina: 5%, Chile: 4%, Other: 3.5%

**Stage breakdown (2025):**
- Pre-seed / Seed: 52% of deals by count, 18% of capital
- Series A: 28% of deals, 31% of capital
- Series B+: 20% of deals, 51% of capital
- Pre-seed deals: down 39% since 2022 peak `(LATAM, LAVCA 2025)`

**Median check sizes (2025):**
- Pre-seed: $300k–$750k `(LATAM, LAVCA 2025)`
- Seed: $1.5M `(LATAM, LAVCA 2025)`
- Series A: $8–12M `(LATAM, LAVCA 2025)`

**Round timing (LATAM vs US):**
- Median time from Seed to Series A in LATAM: 20 months `(LATAM, LAVCA 2025)`
  US equivalent: 15 months `(US-sourced benchmark, a16z / Carta 2025)`
- Implication: LATAM fund deployment timelines are longer. 5-year investment
  period requires starting deployment in Year 1 — do not wait for "perfect timing."

## Regulatory Environment

### Mexico

| Regulator | Jurisdiction | LATAM Implication for Thesis |
|-----------|-------------|-------------------------------|
| CNBV (Comisión Nacional Bancaria y de Valores) | Fintech, banking, securities | Fintech license timeline: 18–36 months from application. SOFOM structure is common workaround (60-day setup, limited license). |
| COFEPRIS | Healthtech, pharmaceuticals | Health product approvals: 24–48 months. Telehealth regulations still evolving post-COVID. |
| SAT (Secretaría de Administración Tributaria) | Tax, invoicing compliance | CFDI mandate creates compliance risk for companies that don't build SAT integration natively |
| Banco de México (BANXICO) | Payments, FX regulation | CoDi / SPEI real-time payment infrastructure access requires BANXICO authorization |

**Thesis implication:** Mexico fintech companies with SOFOM structures have 18-month
regulatory timeline advantage vs. full CNBV license applicants. Fund should require
regulatory structure as closing condition on all fintech investments.

### Brazil

| Regulator | Jurisdiction | LATAM Implication for Thesis |
|-----------|-------------|-------------------------------|
| BACEN (Banco Central do Brasil) | Fintech, payments, open banking | PIX / open banking access requires registration. IP (Instituição de Pagamento) license: 12–24 months. SCD / SEP structures available for credit. |
| ANVISA | Health products, medical devices | Medical device approval: 18–36 months. Software as Medical Device (SaMD) framework still developing in Brazil. |
| CVM (Comissão de Valores Mobiliários) | Capital markets, securities | Investment platforms require CVM certification. Crowdfunding regulations formalized 2023. |
| LGPD (Lei Geral de Proteção de Dados) | Data protection | Affects all B2B SaaS companies storing Brazilian personal data. DPA required for processing. |

**Thesis implication:** Brazilian fintech companies face more complex regulatory
paths than Mexico equivalent due to BACEN licensing requirements. SCD (Sociedade de
Crédito Direto) structure enables credit without full banking license but requires
minimum BRL 1M capital reserve.

## DFI LP Considerations

**DFI LPs in LATAM VC:** IDB Invest, CAF (Development Bank of Latin America),
OPIC/DFC (US International Development Finance Corporation), FMO (Dutch), Proparco (French).

**What DFI LPs require in the investment thesis:**
1. **Development additionality:** How does this fund's investment thesis create development
   impact that would not occur through commercial capital alone?
2. **Impact metrics:** Expected number of underbanked individuals reached, SMEs financed,
   jobs created directly or indirectly through portfolio companies.
3. **ESG alignment:** Portfolio company governance standards, gender lens metrics,
   environmental risk assessment.
4. **Additionality justification:** Why does the target market need DFI-backed capital
   rather than pure commercial VC?

**Standard DFI thesis language (adapt for each fund):**
"[Fund name]'s portfolio targets fintech and SaaS companies serving the 80% of LATAM
SMEs currently excluded from formal credit markets. These markets have structural market
failures — information asymmetry, high transaction costs, regulatory complexity — that
commercial capital has not resolved in 30 years of fintech investment. DFI co-investment
provides the blended finance structure that makes early-stage thesis investments viable
at fund sizes below $50M, enabling a GP team that cannot raise from US institutional
LPs alone to build a differentiated portfolio."

## Family Office LP Alignment

LATAM VC funds at the $15M–$50M range are primarily backed by family offices
(LATAM and US based) rather than institutional LPs (endowments, pension funds).

**What family office LPs prioritize (vs. institutional LPs):**
- Relationship and trust with GP: longer evaluation period, more weight on GP track record
  vs. institutional LPs who use quantitative screening
- Alignment of values: family offices often want investment thesis aligned with industries
  they understand (banking, real estate, manufacturing — not deep tech)
- Portfolio diversification function: family offices may want VC as 5–10% of their portfolio
  for return asymmetry, not as primary investment vehicle
- Governance simplicity: family offices prefer clean LPA structures with minimal side letters

**Thesis adaptation for family office LPs:**
The thesis must speak to: (1) financial return potential (3× fund in 10 years), (2)
LATAM economic alignment (investing in companies that strengthen LATAM economy — family
office reputation), and (3) GP access to deal flow that the family office could not
replicate on its own.

## Exit Environment Context

**LATAM VC exit options (2025):**
- Strategic acquisitions: Primary exit path (accounts for 85%+ of LATAM VC exits by count)
  Acquirers: Global financial services (Nubank, MercadoPago acquiring competitors),
  US/European companies acquiring LATAM distribution
- Secondary sales: Growing, but still limited compared to US markets
- IPOs: Rare — LATAM public market depth insufficient for most VC-backed companies.
  Brazilian B3 has hosted 2 VC-backed tech IPOs since 2022 (vs. 25+ in US in same period)
- US IPO for LATAM companies: Requires 18+ months of US market preparation; realistic
  only for $100M+ ARR companies with international customer base

**Thesis implications:**
- Underwrite to strategic acquisition, not IPO. If the thesis requires an IPO exit
  to return the fund, that is a structural problem — LATAM IPO market is not reliable.
- Name your acquirer thesis in the portfolio construction section. "Who acquires this
  company at a $300M valuation?" must have a specific answer at investment.

## LATAM Discount Annotation Standard

Per D-24 (Phase 2 decisions), all LATAM fund performance benchmarks must be annotated:

- US benchmark: `(US-sourced benchmark, Carta Q1 2026 / Cambridge Associates)`
- LATAM equivalent: `(LATAM — no equivalent public dataset; expect 30–50% discount to US at same vintage)`

**Rationale:** LATAM VC funds at equivalent vintage consistently underperform US VC
medians by 30–50% on TVPI and IRR due to: longer time-to-liquidity, smaller exit market,
higher FX risk on USD-denominated returns, and smaller fund sizes (lower absolute return
on equivalent % carry). This is not a failure — it is a market structure difference
that LPs must understand before committing.
