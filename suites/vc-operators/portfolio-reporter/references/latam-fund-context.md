# LATAM Fund Context (LP Reporting)

Figures with a source are data. Anything marked "directional" is practitioner judgment from
LATAM Fund I practice and must not be presented to LPs as a statistic.

---

## 1. Who reads a LATAM Fund I report (directional)

| LP type | Typical weight in a LATAM Fund I | What they want | ILPA expectation |
|---------|----------------------------------|----------------|------------------|
| Local family offices | Often the majority | A call plus a short written summary | Low; relationship-driven |
| LATAM corporates | Minority | Strategic deal flow, co-invest, annual summary | Low to medium |
| US/EU institutions, funds of funds | Minority | Full ILPA templates, capital account | High |
| DFIs (IDB Invest, IDB Lab, IFC, CAF) | 0 to a large anchor share | Semi-annual formal report, audit, impact metrics | Very high, plus impact |

Rule: produce the full ILPA-style report as the baseline, then a one-page summary for family
offices. Simplify the presentation, never the data.

## 2. What LATAM LPs ask that US LPs often do not

1. **FX:** exposure of portfolio companies with USD costs and local revenue. Quote the actual
   quarter move of BRL, MXN, COP or ARS against USD from a market data source.
2. **Regulation:** open finance (BCB), fintech law (CNBV), data protection (LGPD, LFPDPPP,
   Habeas Data) when it moves a portfolio company's timeline.
3. **Co-investment:** family offices often want co-invest rights in winners; say when one opens.
4. **A reference frame:** many have no VC benchmark at all. Give Carta with the US label plus
   the LATAM caveat below.

## 3. Exit environment (sourced)

Cuantico VP, "Latin America VC Report 2026" (full-year 2025):
https://reports.cuanticovp.com/latin-america-vc-report-2026/
- VC-backed exits: US$4.9B across 63 transactions in 2025, up 172% from 2024 (62 transactions).
- Main exits: M&A 67%, secondaries 27%, one SPAC. Two deals were about 48% of disclosed value.
- Investor survey (126 GPs and LPs): 62.7% call the exit environment unfavorable; 73.8% name
  lack of exits as the top 2026 risk.

US contrast: 1,578 US VC exits in 2025, 50 of them IPOs (PitchBook-NVCA Venture Monitor Q1 2026).
Do not present IPO as the base case for a LATAM Fund I company.

## 4. DPI expectations (sourced, US)

Median net DPI by vintage, Carta "VC Fund Performance: Q2 2026" (data through 30 Jun 2026):
2017: 0.37x, 2018: 0.15x, 2019: 0.04x, 2020: 0.02x, 2021-2024: 0x. Even the 75th percentile
of 2017 funds is at 0.70x after about nine years.

Use these to set expectations: if US funds take nine-plus years to return capital at the
median, a LATAM fund with a thinner exit market should not promise faster. Include a DPI
outlook paragraph in every report until DPI passes 0.5x.

## 5. Standard Carta annotation (paste whenever Carta appears)

> (US-sourced benchmark, Carta Q2 2026): US-domiciled funds only. No public LATAM VC vintage
> benchmark of comparable scale exists; Cambridge Associates' Emerging Markets PE & VC index
> (10-year pooled IRR 8.12% as of 31 Mar 2026) is the closest free reference. LATAM exits
> concentrate in M&A and secondaries (Cuantico 2026), so DPI typically arrives later than in
> US funds.

Never quote a "LATAM funds are X% below US" number: no public dataset supports one.

## 6. Secondaries for watch-list companies (directional)

Secondaries were 27% of the main LATAM exits in 2025 (Cuantico 2026), so a secondary is a
realistic disposition path for a company that cannot raise a primary round. Pricing is
deal-specific: report the actual bid vs last-round price rather than a market "discount".
