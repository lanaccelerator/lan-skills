## LATAM LP Base

**Primary LP archetypes in LATAM fund I:**

| LP Type | Typical % of LATAM Fund I | Reporting Preferences | ILPA Compliance Expectation |
|---------|--------------------------|----------------------|--------------------------|
| Family offices (local) | 50–70% | Quarterly calls preferred over written reports; simplified format | Low — governance is informal; relationship-driven |
| Corporate LPs (LATAM) | 10–25% | Annual summary + quarterly call | Low — LP committee may not review written reports |
| Institutional LPs (US/EU) | 5–15% | Full ILPA-compliant written reports | High — ILPA expected; deviation requires explanation |
| Development finance institutions (IFC, IADB, etc.) | 0–20% | Semi-annual formal reporting + annual audit | Very high — full ILPA + impact reporting |
| Family offices (US/EU) | 5–10% | Written quarterly + annual call | Medium — appreciate ILPA structure but not required |

**Implication:** Most LATAM fund I GPs serve a mixed LP base where institutional ILPA compliance is
required for a minority of LPs. Best practice: produce ILPA-compliant full report as baseline,
offer simplified one-pager for family office LPs. Never simplify the underlying data — only
the presentation format.

**Family office LP dynamics specific to LATAM:**
- Many LATAM family office LPs have limited VC fund exposure — this may be their first fund
- Quarterly written reports may go unread; GP should confirm LP engagement level per LP
- Relationship and personal communication are primary trust signals — not reporting format
- Do not use US-style LPAC structure language with family offices who have no legal representation

## LATAM Fund Reporting Norms

**What LATAM LPs ask about that US institutional LPs typically do not:**

1. **FX exposure:** Brazilian and Mexican LPs invested in USD-denominated funds ask about
   portfolio company FX exposure (USD costs vs local revenue). Include FX environment in
   every market context section when applicable.

2. **Regulatory news:** LATAM regulatory shifts (CNBV fintech, BACEN open finance, LGPD)
   affect portfolio company timelines. LPs follow this news and expect GPs to address it.

3. **Co-investment access:** LATAM family office LPs often want co-investment rights in
   top-performing companies. Proactively mention co-investment opportunities in the report.

4. **Comparative context:** LATAM LPs may not have benchmarks for fund performance.
   Include Carta context (with proper US-sourced label) plus LATAM-specific interpretation.

**What to avoid:**
- Do not cite US exit timelines as reference without LATAM adjustment
- Do not reference IPO as primary exit path — B3 IPO threshold is $100M+ ARR; most
  LATAM fund I companies exit via M&A or secondary
- Do not present ILPA metrics without explanation if LP base has low institutional experience

## LATAM Exit Environment

**Exit pathway comparison:**

| Exit Type | US Fund I (typical) | LATAM Fund I (typical) |
|-----------|--------------------|-----------------------|
| IPO | 5–10% of exits | <2% — B3/NYSE threshold too high for most; requires $100M+ ARR |
| Strategic M&A | 40–50% of exits | 30–40% — limited acquirer pool; mostly US/EU strategics |
| Secondary (fund to fund) | 15–20% | 10–15% — thin secondary market; fewer LATAM fund buyers |
| PE buyout | 15–25% | 20–30% — Brazilian PE active; less so in Mexico/Colombia |
| Write-off | 15–25% | 20–30% — LATAM regulatory/FX risk increases failure rate |

**Exit timeline:**
LATAM M&A typically 18–36 months longer than equivalent US exits due to:
- Fewer acquirers with LATAM acquisition experience
- Currency conversion complexity for cross-border M&A
- Regulatory approval timelines (CADE in Brazil, COFECE in Mexico)
- Smaller acquirer market (no equivalent to FAANG/Microsoft acquisition pipeline)

**DPI expectations by fund year:**

| Fund Year | US Fund I Typical DPI | LATAM Fund I Expectation |
|-----------|----------------------|--------------------------|
| Year 3–4 | 0.0–0.2× | 0.0–0.1× — first secondaries may appear |
| Year 5–6 | 0.2–0.5× | 0.1–0.3× — M&A/secondary phase begins |
| Year 7–8 | 0.5–1.0× | 0.3–0.7× — harvest period |
| Year 9–10 | 1.0–2.0× | 0.7–1.5× — assuming no J-curve extension |

**LP communication guidance:** Set DPI expectations in every report until DPI exceeds 0.5×.
Explicitly state: "LATAM exit timelines are structurally longer than US equivalents. The fund's
harvest period (Years 7–10) is the primary DPI window." Do not leave this unsaid.

## Carta Benchmark Context for LATAM Reports

**Source:** Carta Fund Performance Report Q1 2026. 2,906 US-domiciled funds. Data through Q4 2025.

**CRITICAL:** Carta benchmarks are US-domiciled funds ONLY. Presenting Carta as a global
standard misstates the benchmark audience. Always include the LATAM adjustment note.

**Standard LATAM annotation (required in every report that references Carta):**

> "(US-sourced benchmark, Carta Q1 2026) — US-domiciled funds only. No equivalent LATAM
> fund performance database exists at Carta scale. LAVCA tracks headline LATAM VC investment
> data but does not publish fund-level IRR/TVPI/DPI benchmarks by vintage. LATAM fund
> performance typically 30–50% below US equivalents at same vintage due to longer exit
> timelines, thinner secondary markets, and fewer Series A+ follow-on funds to drive markups."

**When to use Carta benchmarks in LATAM reports:**
- Use to give LPs a reference frame for what US fund performance looks like at same vintage
- Position LATAM fund as "performing at or above LATAM peer expectations" — not "above US median"
- If LATAM fund is outperforming Carta US median, note this explicitly as strong performance
- Do not use Carta benchmarks without the LATAM annotation

## Secondary Market Note

**LATAM secondary market (2025):**
- Thin — fewer buyers than US equivalent
- Primary buyers: other LATAM VC funds (limited), US-based VC funds with LATAM exposure
- Secondary transaction timelines: 3–6 months (vs 4–8 weeks in US)
- Pricing discount: 15–30% to last round vs 0–10% discount in US secondaries
- Momentum: Activity increasing post-2023; Kaszek, Advent, and DILA Capital are active buyers

**For watch list companies:** Secondary sale is a realistic exit for companies that cannot
raise new primary rounds. Include secondary market context when recommending disposition
for watch list companies.
