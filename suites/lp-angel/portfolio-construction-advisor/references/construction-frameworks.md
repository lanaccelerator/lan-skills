## The Power Law — Foundation of Portfolio Construction

**Source:** Correlation Ventures, analysis of 6,000+ investments

The central fact of venture investing:

- Top 10% of companies generate >90% of returns
- Top 1% of companies (1 in 100) produce returns that can return an entire portfolio
- The median venture investment returns less than the capital invested

**Implication:** Portfolio construction is not about finding multiple good companies. It is
about building a portfolio large enough that you reliably capture at least one power law
outlier — and having enough ownership in that outlier to matter.

**The math:**
- 20-company portfolio: probability of including a 10x+ company ≈ 18% (based on historical
  venture base rates)
- 30-company portfolio: probability increases to ~26%
- 50-company portfolio: probability increases to ~40%
- The outlier probability compounds with portfolio size, but ownership per deal is fixed by
  check size — this is the core tension

## Optimal Portfolio Size by Return Target

| Return Target | Minimum Companies | Recommended Companies | Reasoning |
|---------------|------------------|----------------------|-----------|
| 2x portfolio (1.5x–2.5x range) | 12 | 15–20 | Achievable with 2–3 strong performers; power law less critical |
| 3x portfolio | 20 | 25–30 | Requires 1 fund-returner (10x+) and 3–5 strong performers |
| 5x portfolio | 25 | 30–40 | Requires 1 fund-returner (15x+) and 5–8 strong performers; diversification critical |
| 10x portfolio | 40 | 50+ | Requires 1–2 generational outliers; only accessible through scale |

**LATAM adjustment:** At 12% angel-to-Series A conversion (vs 22% US), reach the same follow-on
optionality requires ~1.8x the company count vs a US-equivalent portfolio. A 20-company US
portfolio needs ~36 LATAM companies to produce equivalent Series A conversion probability.
(LATAM, LAVCA 2025)

## Check Size and Ownership Math

**Target ownership at entry:** 1–5% is typical for angel investors; 5–10% for seed funds.
Higher ownership = higher power law capture when the outlier exits.

**Check size examples (illustrative, 2026 LATAM pricing):**

| Stage | Typical LATAM Pre-Money | $50k Check Ownership | $100k Check Ownership | $250k Check Ownership |
|-------|------------------------|---------------------|----------------------|----------------------|
| Pre-seed | $2M–$5M | 1.0%–2.5% | 2.0%–5.0% | 5.0%–12.5% |
| Seed | $5M–$15M | 0.3%–1.0% | 0.7%–2.0% | 1.7%–5.0% |
| Series A | $15M–$40M | 0.1%–0.3% | 0.25%–0.7% | 0.6%–1.7% |

**Dilution model over life of investment:**
- Typical dilution from Seed to exit: 60–75% for a LATAM startup with 3–4 follow-on rounds
- $100k at $8M Seed pre-money = 1.25% ownership
- After Series A, B, C dilution (70% typical): 0.375% ownership at exit
- At $200M exit: $750k gross return = 7.5x on $100k invested (before carry and fees)

**Implication:** Check size must be set at entry with dilution modeled — not just ownership
at entry. An angel who does not follow on loses ownership with every new round.

## Reserve Ratio Design

**Standard recommendation:** 30–40% of total portfolio capital reserved for follow-on investments.

| Reserve Ratio | Portfolio Behavior | Best For |
|---------------|-------------------|----------|
| 10–15% | Minimal follow-on; primarily first-check strategy | Angel investors who do not have pro-rata rights; very early stage (pre-seed) |
| 25–35% | Moderate follow-on; participate in best performers' next rounds | Angels with pro-rata rights; seed-stage focused |
| 40–50% | Active follow-on; concentrate into winners as they emerge | Family offices; small fund strategy |

**Reserve math example ($3M portfolio):**
- 35% reserve = $1.05M reserved for follow-ons
- $1.95M for initial checks
- At $100k avg check = ~20 initial investments
- Follow-on into top 4–5 performers: $210k–$262k per follow-on at Seed-to-A

**Critical requirement:** Pro-rata rights must be negotiated at entry. Without pro-rata rights,
the reserve ratio is meaningless — the company can raise its next round without offering you
participation. For LATAM Seed deals, pro-rata rights are not always standard and require
explicit negotiation.

## Stage Allocation

**Standard angel portfolio stage mix:**

| Stage | Recommended Allocation | Risk Profile | Ownership Math |
|-------|----------------------|--------------|----------------|
| Pre-seed | 50–60% of company count | Highest risk, highest potential ownership | Entry ownership 2–5% before dilution |
| Seed | 30–40% of company count | Moderate risk; some validation | Entry ownership 1–2% |
| Series A | 10–20% of company count | Lower risk; de-risked business | Entry ownership 0.5–1% |

**LATAM note:** LATAM pre-seed deal volume contracted 77% from 2022 peak (660 deals) to 2025
(152 deals). Pre-seed deals are scarcer and accelerator-sourced deals have become the primary
pre-seed entry point. (Cuantico 2026)

## Concentrated vs Diversified — The Thiel Debate

**Peter Thiel's view (Zero to One):** Make 1–3 high-conviction bets. Diversification is an
admission you do not know which bet will win. If you know, concentrate. If you do not know,
you should not be investing.

**AngelList data view:** At scale (500+ angel portfolios analyzed), diversified portfolios
(25+ companies) outperform concentrated portfolios (≤5 companies) in median returns, primarily
because concentrated portfolios fail to capture a power law outlier in most cases.

**LATAM resolution:** Thiel's framework assumes you have information advantage and access to
the best deals. Most LATAM angels do not have consistent access to the top 1% of deals. The
AngelList diversification argument applies: without information edge, diversification is the
correct strategy. For LATAM angels specifically, the lower base rate of success (12% angel-
to-Series A vs 22% US) makes concentration more dangerous, not less.

**Practical guideline:** Concentrate only if you have: (1) proprietary deal flow, (2) verified
information advantage on a specific sector, and (3) >5 years of direct investing experience.
Otherwise, diversify.

## Return Distribution Model

What a well-constructed 25-company LATAM angel portfolio produces (expected value, not guaranteed):

| Outcome Tier | # Companies | Expected Return | Total Return Contribution |
|-------------|-------------|-----------------|--------------------------|
| Fund-returner (10x+) | 1 | $X total × 15x avg | Returns entire portfolio + profit |
| Strong performers (3–8x) | 3–4 | $X total × 5x avg | 2–3x contribution |
| Modest returns (1–2x) | 5–7 | $X total × 1.5x avg | Portfolio cost recovery |
| Write-offs (0–0.5x) | 12–15 | ~$0 | Full loss on capital deployed |

**Note:** This is an expected value model, not a guarantee. The write-off rate for LATAM
angel investments is higher than US due to lower Series A+ funding availability.
